## 1 · Every turn must move something

A line earns its place when it changes at least one of:
- knowledge;
- power/status;
- relationship;
- intention;
- possibility;
- tension;
- interpretation of the current event.

A line that merely restates visible state is usually cut.

Small talk is allowed when it performs a social job:
- tests distance;
- establishes local colour;
- hides or leaks a want;
- marks familiarity;
- creates contrast before a meaningful beat.

Small talk is not filler.

## 2 · THEREFORE / BUT at line scale

Use NIE/South-Park causality not only scene-to-scene but turn-to-turn.

Good exchange logic:
`claim → THEREFORE response → BUT complication → THEREFORE changed position`

Avoid:
`remark → and then another remark → and then a joke`

For a Triplet, Subject / Connector / Reframe should form a causal or adversarial movement, not three compatible fragments placed side by side.

## 3 · SAID / MEANT / WANTED

For any important line, author or audit three layers:

- **SAID** — literal utterance.
- **MEANT** — subtext / interpretation.
- **WANTED** — what the speaker wants the other person or world to do.

At least one of MEANT/WANTED should be inferable from behavior, timing, relationship or context rather than explained aloud.

This maps directly to the historical NIE/Overworld `tell` concept: the hidden want may leak through gesture, gaze, Emanata, silence or timing.

## 4 · Adjacency pairs create playable pressure

Treat exchanges as calls that demand a response:
- question → answer / evasion / refusal / redirect;
- accusation → denial / admission / counter-accusation;
- request → acceptance / rejection / negotiation;
- offer → acceptance / suspicion / counter-offer;
- compliment → acknowledgment / deflection / distrust.

The interesting beat is often the **broken expected response**.

Silence, non-sequitur and interruption are not missing content when they reveal character or power.

## 5 · Silence is a valid authored result

Do not manufacture a line because a Resident is present.

Allowed performance outputs include:
- speech;
- speech + gaze;
- gaze only;
- gesture only;
- pause;
- silence.

Use silence when:
- the expected adjacency-pair response would reveal too much;
- power is expressed by refusing to answer;
- realization needs landing space;
- the visual/performance reaction is stronger than text;
- repetition would turn a beat into commentary on itself.

This aligns with current KFB Resident Performance: Bubble/Speech is one channel among several, not the sole carrier of meaning.

## 6 · Interruption is characterization

An interruption must reveal **why this speaker takes the floor now**.

Possible functions:
- seize status;
- protect someone;
- avoid an answer;
- correct a false premise;
- exploit an opening;
- redirect embarrassment;
- puncture a ritual;
- prevent escalation.

Do not sprinkle interruptions for tempo alone.

The interrupted speaker's reaction is part of the beat.

## 7 · Voice comes from worldview, not catchphrases

Character voice dimensions:
- vocabulary/register;
- sentence length;
- directness;
- willingness to answer;
- preferred evidence;
- certainty/hedging pattern;
- local/faction vocabulary;
- interruption tendency;
- repair style after misunderstanding;
- what the speaker never says;
- silence behavior.

Historical NIE rule:
**voice is sociolect + worldview**.

Swap test:
If two Residents can exchange the same line without changing its meaning, the line is probably functional rather than character-specific.

Do not solve voice by recurring catchphrase spam.

## 8 · Tics and tells belong to meaning

A tic/tell is useful only when it:
- expresses an inner contradiction;
- leaks a concealed want;
- changes under stress;
- marks a relationship;
- creates a readable pattern the player can learn.

Examples of channels, not fixed animations:
- gaze avoidance;
- delayed look;
- one recurring hand/shoulder action;
- posture shift;
- speech repair;
- unfinished sentence;
- repeated checking of an object.

Semantic meaning remains in Resident/ChatterBox data. Blender/Face/EyeRig only perform the cue.

## 9 · Follow people, not props

A Card, relic, shop item, vehicle or POI becomes narratively useful through **what someone wants from it**.

Ask:
- Who wants this?
- Who fears it?
- Who profits from it?
- Who remembers it differently?
- Who lies about it?
- What relationship changes because of it?

Do not make the prop explain itself unless the actual KFB concept explicitly makes that object a character.

## 10 · One concrete picture before abstraction

Prefer:
- observable action;
- named place;
- physical consequence;
- specific social behavior;
- concrete object in a person's hands.

Before a philosophical/general line, require one concrete anchor whenever possible.

This is the KFB/NIE “every word pays rent” rule applied to game dialogue.

## 11 · Straight-face Kayfabe

The stranger the proposition, the less the line should beg the audience to notice the joke.

Default:
- no wink;
- no generic meta-game joke;
- no “this is weird” commentary;
- no AI/system/glitch joke;
- no ornamental absurdity.

Characters behave as if their worldview is locally coherent.
Humor emerges from consequence, contradiction, precision and social behavior.

## 12 · Failure reveals character

Use failure/revival/defeat as a character probe, not an automatic punchline trigger.

Ask:
- What did this Resident expect the player to do?
- What changes in their estimate of the player?
- Does sympathy, contempt, concern, opportunism or rivalry rise?
- What old interaction becomes newly relevant?
- What excuse does the player/Resident implicitly offer?
- Who in a group speaks, and who chooses not to?

Repeated failure should increase **specificity and memory**, not merely joke intensity.

## 13 · Memory earns callbacks

A callback is strong when the old beat acquires new meaning.

Memory candidates:
- a prior promise;
- a previous failure;
- a gift/trade;
- a boast;
- a saved/ignored Resident;
- a Card/POI both actors witnessed;
- an earlier misunderstanding.

Avoid callbacks that exist only to prove the system remembers.

## 14 · Group reactions need role selection

When several Residents witness an event, do not let everyone emit a line.

Choose:
- primary respondent;
- supporter/opponent;
- silent observer;
- optional interrupter.

Selection may depend on:
- relationship;
- status;
- proximity;
- current activity;
- information;
- temperament;
- who has the strongest stake.

This fits the current KFB performance budget and prevents subtitle-wall chatter.

## 15 · Local colour must change language, not add decoration

Place/faction identity should affect:
- what examples are normal;
- what is considered embarrassing;
- what counts as evidence;
- who is addressed formally;
- what local institutions/POIs matter;
- what metaphors arise naturally from actual activity.

Do not paste dialect spelling or decorative jargon onto generic lines.

## 16 · Writer's Room use

Use NIE Writer's Room as an **authoring method**, not an in-game ensemble.

Recommended compact pass:
1. **Open break / Yes-And** — generate options without early rejection.
2. **Conflict pass** — identify want, obstacle, stake.
3. **Character web pass** — identify relationship/power/history.
4. **Therefore/But pass** — connect beats causally.
5. **Voice pass** — worldview/register/silence/interruption.
6. **Concrete-image pass** — replace abstraction with observable specifics.
7. **Selection pass** — room runner/editor chooses.
8. **Anti-slop pass** — remove generic AI humor, explanation and catchphrase repetition.

Multiple NIE voices may be used internally to challenge material.
Do not expose Writer's Room personas as NPCs unless independently authored as actual KFB characters.

## 17 · Context budget

Historical NIE adapter rule retained as a strong design principle:

**A bubble gets only as much world as its beat can pay for.**

Context priority:
1. current Card/semantic seed;
2. immediate situation;
3. speaker worldview/faction;
4. relationship;
5. local history/memory;
6. broader/global context.

A short local reaction should not summarize the whole lore corpus.

## 18 · Acceptance audit for one candidate line

PASS only if the line can answer:
- What moved?
- What does the speaker want?
- Why this speaker?
- Why now?
- Could silence/gesture do it better?
- Is the voice distinguishable?
- Is the line concrete enough?
- Does it connect by THEREFORE/BUT to the previous/next beat?
- Is any joke played straight?
- Is the line short enough for the actual carrier?

If two or more answers are weak, rewrite or cut.
