## S1 · BEETLE RUMBLE · KFB signature / opener

**Suggested BPM:** 116–118  
**Suggested tonal center:** D Mixolydian / D minor-pentatonic blues color

118 BPM instrumental psychedelic rockabilly electro-funk opener with cosmic surf energy, a danceable cartoon-series swagger and a slightly ridiculous beetle-wrestling entrance feel. Start immediately with a short, unforgettable 5–7 note signature hook in D Mixolydian / minor-pentatonic blues color, stated by spring-reverb twang guitar and clipped clavinet in tight unison. Drive it with punchy dry rockabilly kick/snare, occasional tom fills, restrained shaker and hand percussion, a buoyant slap/boogie electric-bass groove, muted funk-guitar scratches, warm Wurlitzer/Rhodes stabs, Hammond-style organ support and small analog electro-funk synth answers. Let the same hook reappear in different colors — bass, organ, synth or short brass punches — so it remains recognizable even when most layers drop out. Structure in clear 8/16-bar blocks: immediate hook, main groove, one-beat stop, funkier second statement, short cosmic-surf lift, compact psychedelic disco / wrestling-arena peak, then a decisive final return and clean sting. Exuberant, weird, physical and mischievous rather than cute; catchy enough to work as a series ident, game title cue, victory sting or recurring KFB motif. Use slapback echo, spring reverb, surf tremolo and brief tape/phaser gestures as color only. Preserve negative space for cartoon SFX and dialogue. Instrumental only. No vocals or chants, no xylophone/marimba/vibraphone/bells, no ska or swing-big-band cliché, no pop-punk, no metal shredding, no heroic superhero orchestration, no EDM riser/drop and no glossy trailer boom.

**Stem priority:** Drums, Bass, Guitar, Keyboard/Clavinet/Rhodes/Organ, Percussion, Synth, Brass/Other FX.

**Selection rule:** generate 2–4 masters first. Choose the version with the strongest one-listen hook, best bass/drum pocket, clearest 8/16-bar form, enough SFX space and a hook that still works when reduced to one or two instruments. Export stems only from the human-selected winner.
