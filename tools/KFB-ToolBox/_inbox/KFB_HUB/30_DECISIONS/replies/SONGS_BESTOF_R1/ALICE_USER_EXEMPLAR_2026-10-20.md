## LYRICS · user example

```text
[Spoken Word]

Yo! Are you fluffy?

Tonight we play
my favorite KayfaBizarro
Cut & Play Comic Card Game:
"Alice in ... Blödsinnland!"
[cheering crowd]

Enter the Kayfabe Wonderland court
where the sentence comes before the evidence
and making sense is forbidden.

Court's in session: tonight I read thirty cards out loud while you play the jury.

Ever sat in a meeting where the boss announces the decision, then asks for feedback? Congratulations, you already work in Wonderland.

For the old fans: ten and six on the hat, and the raven riddle still has no answer.

The White Rabbit: late for a very important date. Possibly a hallucination; melting watch.

The Caterpillar demands you explain who you are, then rejects your answer; question-mark smoke.

Rejected, Alice asks: The Cheshire Cat, we're all mad here, I'm just honest about it; fading, huge grin.

The Mad Hatter murdered time; now lives in eternal six in the evening, a weasel spilling tea.

The March Hare, aggressive hospitality; takes best butter very seriously, flinging a teapot.

In that teapot: The Dormouse, asleep: breathing while sleeping is the same as sleeping while breathing.

Nobody sentenced? The Queen of Hearts, verdict first, sentence later; off with their heads! A lioness.

But behind her back: The King of Hearts pardons everyone when the Queen isn't looking; crown over eyes.

The Duchess: everything has a moral, if you can find it; usually involves pepper, and a piglet.

The Visitor, a cryptid ape, changes size rapidly; ask the Dodo for classification.

The Rabbit Hole, gravity optional; cupboards, clocks and a marmalade jar in a falling vortex.

The Pool of Tears, salty; all communication while holding your nose, dodo and mouse in waves.

The Hall of Doors, locked out until you solve a riddle; a golden key on a glass table.

Through that door: The Duchess' Kitchen, pepper sneeze before speaking; flying pans in pepper smoke.

Worse than pepper: The Mad Tea Party, a time loop; long table, empty chairs, last sentence repeated.

The Rose Garden, a cover up: white roses dripping red paint, card soldiers with brushes.

The Croquet Ground, live ammo: flamingo mallets and hedgehog balls that might run away.

Lost at croquet? The High Tribunal, kangaroo court: logic forbidden; if a story makes sense, Blödsinn!

The Mock Turtle's Rock, melancholy; all stories with a deep, heavy sigh, a weeping turtle.

Suspicious Cake: Eat Me. So you eat, you grow, and now you can intimidate the King.

Too tall for the hall: Unknown Bottle, Drink Me. So you drink, you shrink, you hide inside other cards.

Can't pick a size? The Mushroom, a bitten red fly agaric: you grow, you shrink, or a neck like a serpent.

The Vorpal Flamingo, grumpy, with a baseball bat: strike a conversation, change topic.

Talking's over: The Squeaky Slate-Pencil, a slate reading SCREECH; an annoyance: write instead of speak.

The Treacle Well, a sticky trap you cannot leave; teapot and bowler stuck in the pit. Humbug!

Sticky, but hurry: Paint the Roses Red, conceal a mistake before the Queen arrives. Risk, decapitation.

Her herald runs late: Find the Gloves, help the White Rabbit find his accessories; mistaken for Mary Ann.

Solve the Riddle: why is a raven like a writing desk? Risk: no answer, an infinite loop.

No answer, so: Who Stole the Tarts? The Knave is guilty, but the evidence is a poem that makes no sense.

Exhibit A: Give Evidence, recite a poem correctly; the words come out wrong: Twinkle Twinkle Little Bat.

Same deal out here: the Knave goes down on a poem nobody understood, the boss gets pardoned, and the rest of us get called Mary Ann and sent for the gloves.

Feedback welcome, of course: right after the sentence.

Stay Fluffy, Folks!
```
