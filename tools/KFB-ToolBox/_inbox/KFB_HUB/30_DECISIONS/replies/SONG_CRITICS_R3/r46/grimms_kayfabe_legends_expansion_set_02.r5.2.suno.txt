Grimms' Kayfabe Legends: The Fairy Tale Screwjob, where every happy ending was rigged.

Uncle FrizzleBob here, reading fifty-two uncut Grimm tales like wrestling promos.

Mom read you soft Cinderella. Here a sister cuts off her heel to fit the shoe. Black Friday!

The Golden Goose: he who holds the goose holds the heat; everyone else, a hanger-on.

The Lump of Silver: this load hurts my shoulder; horse, cow, pig, then nothing but stone.

The Straw, Coal, and Bean: the impetuous coal tripped boldly; laughter splits the belly seam.

Botch a spot, still get pushed: The Turnip: compared to this vegetable, schools are as empty air.

The Millstone: crash, the bird threw the millstone down on her head; crushed to death.

Smaller kills, bigger belt: The Seven-Fly Girdle: Seven at one stroke; respect for the tiny fellow.

The Poison Comb: Snowdrop's breath was stopped, and she fell down as if she were dead.

The Axe and Anvil: I can do better than that; in it caught the old man's beard. Crime scene!

The Bremen Pyramid: the ass brayed, the dog barked, the cat mewed, and the cock screamed.

The Seven Kids: we will not open the door, you are the wolf; his belly filled with stones.

The Ants and Ducks: we will remember you, one good turn deserves another; fair wages.

The Twelve Princesses: every morning their shoes worn through, as if danced in all night.

Worn shoes, worse reviews: The Talking Mirror: queen, fairest in this land, but Snowdrop is lovelier.

Back at the goats' door, a softer knock: The Chalk: the wolf bought a great lump of chalk, made his voice soft.

Falada's Head, above a gate: alas, if thy mother knew it, sadly, sadly would she rue it.

The Juniper Bird: Kywitt, what a beautiful bird am I; a song so repetitive it causes madness.

The Wicked Stepmother: we must all four die of hunger, you fool; two exiled to save food.

The False Bride: I shall ride upon Falada, you may have my horse; a horse that knows something.

Steal a name, or guess one: Rumpelstiltskin: the devil told you that! Straw into gold; a guessed name tears him apart.

The Cat of Kehrewit: up with the window, fling out my old man; wolf, dog, or fox as new suitor.

The Golden Bird: one feather is of no use to me, I must have the whole bird, alarm and all.

Brothers want the whole flagon too: The Water of Life: they waited till he was fast asleep, gave him bitter sea-water.

The Wishing Cloak: shoot into the midst of them, the cloak will fall too; gone to a mountain.

The Table, Ass, and Stick: gold is good, but a stick that beats people by itself is better.

Eat up, she brought a tape measure: The Gingerbread Cage: scream as he might, it would not help; his finger checked.

The Witch's Oven: push her in, shut the iron door and bolt it; the wrong figure inside.

The Glass Mountain: every time he attempted to climb, he fell back again; a slippery slope.

The false bride's invoice: The Barrel of Nails: dragged by two white horses, street to street till she is dead.

Doctor Knowall: that man knows everything! A diploma, a guilty rooster, sheer luck.

He guesses right, she worries wrong: Clever Elsie: if the pick-axe should fall on his head, have we not reason to weep?

The Three Languages: I have learnt what the dogs say when they bark; treasure in the tower.

Fluent in dog, lost on people: Hans's Logic: friendly eyes on her, so he cut out calves' eyes, threw them in her face.

Iron Hans: my power is great, greater than you think; gold and silver in abundance.

Big body, little respect: The Giant: such a great fellow, and yet cannot even carry the tree! Defeated by cheese.

The Bear Prince: you would not feel me between your teeth; take these two wicked girls!

Skip one invite, lose a century: The Thirteenth Fairy: not asked to the feast, she set to work to take her revenge.

The Salad: scarcely were they swallowed when she lost her own form; now she can only bray.

The Blue Light: strike down to earth that false judge there, and spare not the king.

Another salad, a worse contract: Rampion: she would have died if she had not got some to eat; her first born for leaves.

The White Snake: the king never took off the cover to eat until alone; gossip of ducks and ants.

The Golden Hair: he tied his handkerchief round his head so the man might not see it.

The Hazel Tree: shake, shake, hazel-tree, gold and silver over me; rags into a ball gown.

Here's the Black Friday sister: The Severed Heel: cut the heel off; as queen you will no longer have to go on foot.

The Peas in Ashes: the good ones go into the pot, the bad ones into your crop; no ball yet.

The Magic Wand: take away her magic wand first, or we cannot escape; a lake and a flower.

Still fleeing, she leaves an alibi: The Drops of Blood: here, on the stairs, I am sweeping; three drops delay the pursuer.

The turnip's line, recycled: The Sack of Wisdom: compared to this seat, schools are as empty air.

The Wishing Ring: you shall have a son with the power of wishing; exactly what you wish for.

The rampion baby grew up: Rapunzel's Braid: Rapunzel, Rapunzel, let down your hair to me; scissors below.

Another climber, no braid: The Fisherman's Wife: my wife Ilsabill will have her own will; throne, clouds, pigsty.

Here's the uninvited fairy's revenge: Sleeping Beauty: the thorns laid hold of them; they stuck fast and died wretchedly.

Cat-Skin: good for nothing but scullion-girl, boots and shoes thrown at my head; golden dress.

Two centuries on, we still cut the heel to fit the shoe. Now we pay for the knife in installments.
