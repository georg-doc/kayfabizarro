# KFB Game Loop R16 · You Choose Something, Invite the Consequences
2026-10-29 · Georg creative steering · ADDITIVE EDITORIAL DESIGN, NOT RUNTIME OR NEW SSOT

**Owner:** existing KFB_HUB / 30_DECISIONS/replies concept lane, Draft PR #460. **Creative acceptance:** Georg/WS0, within the current Bigger Picture / Global Intent. R16 adds a consequential-world principle to the R15 anti-teleology clarification; it does not silently supersede the existing global master, authorize gameplay, or merge other owners.

## 1 · Motto and Greater Intent
**“You choose something, invite the consequences.”**

A player may play a Card, perform a Kayfabe role, voice a position through a character, cover a statement with Goo, expose a Card, give it to an NPC, perform a song or disregard the entire scene. Each action can produce **concrete, locally plausible reactions** by particular people or institutions, changes in material circumstances and future memories.

**The game must not evaluate the ideological correctness of that choice or predesign the player’s supposed growth.** An opinion is neither a player achievement nor a psychological diagnosis. Georg’s humanistic hope that play *could* foster curiosity, mutual recognition, intellectual flexibility or critical reflection remains the creator’s own motivation, **not a reward policy, scripted learning outcome or invisible conversion funnel**.

## 2 · Four axes remain distinct
- **Action:** what actually happened: a Card was played, Ink was applied, a facade was perforated, a gift accepted/refused, a claim voiced or a physical object rebuilt.
- **Presentation/role:** how it was performed and mediated: Babyface, Heel, Man in Black, pop song, FrizzleBob promo, news bulletin, HyperNormalisation collage, shadow theatre. Role and montage are not fixed moral classes.
- **Witness and interpretation:** which actor perceived what, given their own Card knowledge, worldview, relation, exposure history and scene situation. Multiple interpretations can coexist; no omniscient faction opinion.
- **Consequences over time:** local reactions, relationships, access, voluntary cooperation or disagreement, built-world state, reputation, next encounters and ordered receipt memory. The canonical Card source remains unchanged; memory of a claim is not consent to or proof of the claim.

A character’s unpleasant reaction to the player is **a situated NPC response, not the game’s author issuing a verdict**. Player-facing feedback must never secretly transform dissent into bad gameplay or agreement into an XP multiplier.

## 3 · Consequences as an open interaction ecology
The same physical surface or Card can be claimed by multiple performers. A Men in Black official may consider a blackened Card safely contained; a Resident may be relieved, offended or indifferent; a third may attempt to pierce the facade; the player might help either, stage a show, walk away or return tomorrow. A disagreement may create dialogue, a fight, an ongoing social rift, a temporary truce, a joke, a failed encounter or no visible change. **Do not force a reconciliation or inevitable escape from Plato’s Cave.**

This accommodates divergent HyperNormalisation media frames: one montage obscures a claim behind soothing official media; another highlights contradiction while producing its own theatrical bias. They can each alienate or convince particular Residents. **Black Goo vs coloured Fluff is not truth vs falsehood**, and rebuilding an Ink-covered wall is not necessarily evil while tearing it down is not necessarily good.

Causal visibility matters: show a recognizable gesture, retreat, sign replacement, changed Card visibility, crowd response, relationship boundary, or other world effect instead of a generic “wrong answer” toast. Reactions must connect to an authored interest, situation, social context or witness history. Not every choice should trigger an elaborate event.

## 4 · Cartoon biofeedback as a metaphor only
Georg’s “biofeedback” analogy means **immediate embodied, audiovisual response from the fictional gameworld**: squash/stretch, facial/eye reactions, posture, sound, short Triplet, clay deformation, Ink peel, Fluff bounce, crowd sound, music edit, silence or an altered routine.

It **does not** mean sensing or classifying a real player’s beliefs, physiology, emotions, ideology, tolerance, openness or cognitive state. No covert real-user profiling, training telemetry, political segmentation or behavioural optimization. The experience can be cathartic, funny, abrasive, mysterious or quietly unresolved without proving a psychological effect.

Keep performance readable and chill: feedback proportional to the action, genuine opt-out, reduced-motion/skip for rapid collages, no compulsory argument. “Juicy” is the feel of action, not a mandate for sensory saturation.

## 5 · Architecture reuse, not a second simulation
Use the existing Card registry/Almanac for canonical `deckId:cardNumber` and original page/PDF; existing Resident Knowledge, ChatterBox and Lean Event/Social Memory for what was witnessed, said, remembered and shared; existing WS1 World/Combat/Destruction/Fluff state owners for physical objects; current HyperNorm/Media/Audio/Motion owners for presentation.

**Conceptual event path, not implementation schema:**
`CHOICE / ACTION → LOCAL PHYSICAL STATE → OBSERVERS / SITUATED INTERPRETATIONS → VISIBLE REACTIONS → EVENT RECEIPTS / RELATIONSHIP MEMORY → NEW OPTIONAL AFFORDANCES`.

All branches are contingent: some NPCs are not present, some do not care, some remember without changing opinion. Different time/order of exposure (R5 Signal Memory) can make the same final Card set produce a different social history. Persist only meaningful world/encounter events by the actual store owner, not whole conversations or inferred player convictions.

Avoid a new global enlightenment score, debate meter, political alignment classifier, player morality bar, simulation-wide balance target or authored consequence ladder that privileges the designers' preferred interpretation.

## 6 · Review discipline: detect hidden instruction
A reviewer should ask:
1. Does the consequence arise from a **particular character, institution or material affordance**, or merely the author’s wish to reward good thinking?
2. Can playing an antagonist, refusing a discussion, remaining in the cave or choosing an unpopular reading still yield legitimate, enjoyable play?
3. Does the fiction preserve responsibility and independently affected NPCs without turning the game itself into a judge?
4. Is there a visible cause-and-effect response while **the player's interpretation remains open**?
5. Would a player with an unexpected interpretation still recognize the encounter as a coherent game rather than a manipulation?

**Evaluation target:** understandable causal feedback, believable differentiated NPC reactions, comedic/tactile play, meaningful save/revisit continuity and absence of coerced conclusions. Not alleged changes in player's democratic values or tolerance.

## 7 · Source and decision status
**Georg’s design decision at concept level:** the motto “You choose something, invite the consequences” is the **active interpretive complement** to R15 “Universe in a Nutshell / No Philosopher King.” Choice has consequences, **but the game does not assign an enlightened destination**. This is about a more open simulation of human positions and interactions, not neutralizing history, harm or factual provenance.
