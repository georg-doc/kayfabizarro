## Purpose and thesis of the deck
**Title (registry):** "Uncle FrizzleBob's 1001 Kayfabe Nights", a stand-alone expansion for the Krazy Kayfabizarro Komik Kard Klub. deckType "Narrative Wrestling Mashup". Tags: comedy, improv, roleplay, storytelling, #KayfabeIsReal.

**Premise:** *One Thousand and One Nights* is booked as a wrestling territory. Shahryār is the heel promoter who buries a performer every dawn. Scheherazade is the top face booker who "turns the shoot into a work to save the locker room". Every tale is one act on the card: Aladdin, Sinbad, Ali Baba and the thieves, the City of Brass, the Ebony Horse, the Hunchback chain, the Caliph's commission and the Three Apples. The meta-tools close the deck: Suspension of Disbelief, the Montreal Screwjob, the Cliffhanger and the Mask.

**Thesis:** Storytelling is survival kayfabe. The manifesto says "History is a Work. Folklore is a Shoot." The meter card says that if you stop buying the story, you lose the match. A story that never finishes keeps the king from swinging the axe, because the Cliffhanger is "the only defense against the Executioner". The satire points at power. A promoter who treats people as disposable can only be beaten by an audience that has to know what happens next.

**Audience:** Kayfabizarro players who want a story arc with heels, faces and a pink slip on the line. The deck is improv and roleplay rather than conspiracy.

## Arc
- **Build-up:** The song opens on night 1001, mid-story. The king has an axe, the sun is almost up, and Scheherazade asks "where were we?" The court is set up, then three adventure tales run hot (Aladdin, Sinbad, Ali Baba and the thieves), carried by Dunyazad's ringside cues and the "Open sesame!" / "Open... barley?" gag. Cassim forgets his lines and dies, which quietly states the deck's law.
- **Turn (stanzas 8–10):** The City of Brass is the one audience the storyteller cannot work. The King of Brass "cannot be rewritten", the Brass Horseman doesn't care about your story, the Seven Tablets push spoilers, and the Petrified Market is a dead crowd ("Tough crowd."). The reset comes with "New story, sister. Wings!"
- **Recovery and escalation (11–13):** Highbrow failed, so she goes low and the fart gets the laugh ("The king's laughing!"). The Barber's filibuster mirrors her own trick. Then the Executioner arrives: "Clock's ticking."
- **Payoff (14–15 + outro):** The promised apples story finally gets told. At sunrise the boss tries the screwjob, the Cliffhanger stops it ("Not so fast."), the mask stays on. Outro: "Did the king swing? / No. / He has to know what happens next. / To be continued..." The printed power of the Cliffhanger is the last word before "Stay Fluffy!"

## Editorial conventions (not logged per card)
- Card names are written in title case, and the "Power: N" stat tags in the lore field are dropped (stat values are game data, not story).
- The power field is used only as a punchline or setup: Shahryār's "Bore me", Scheherazade's "thousand nights", Dunyazad's apples, the Vizier's "one more night", "Three wishes, no DQ", and the Cliffhanger's "To be continued" in the outro.
- Every word change beyond these conventions is in `songlines.json` (first-person shifts per rule 2, length cuts, spelled-out numbers, removed diacritic, rules jargon).
- Sensitive cards: The Jewish Physician, The African Sorcerer and The Slave are canonical *Nights* characters (Hunchback cycle, Three Apples). Their lines are the printed lore. None gets an opener or a joke about who they are. In wr2 the Slave's added beat ('Blame rolls downhill.') aims at power, and The Black Giant is sung as 'The Giant', 'monster heel' (songLine).
