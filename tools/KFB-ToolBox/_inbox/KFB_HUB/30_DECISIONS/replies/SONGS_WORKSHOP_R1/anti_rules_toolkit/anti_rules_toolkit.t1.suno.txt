[Spoken Word]

The Anti-Rules Manifesto, for people who panic when the adults leave the room.

The Rules Lawyer: Objection! Cite a non-existent rule. If you hesitate, your action fails.

The Optimizer's story is devoid of fun or flavor: nothing breaks faster than trying to weaponize ambiguity.

The Nervous Beginner asks: is this right? Confusing clarity with safety.

The Authority Figure cancels a move as too fun: authority sneaks back in wearing a helpful voice.

The Panic, internal scream: a die rolls off the table and you freeze until someone tells you it's okay.

The Crutch: you cannot take an action unless another player gives you permission first.

The Sweat cannot handle success without questioning it. Others start sweating; this booklet is for them.

The Silence, a controlled void when the table stalls: a moment where players stop asking.

The Lost Winner: give it to anyone discussing Victory Points. Whoever asks how to win has already lost.

The Nonsense State justifies what defies logic but feels right. Blödsinn is not a mistake; it is a state.

The Effective Move ignores a rule if it creates a better story. Nobody is right; some things are effective.

The Amnesia: we start fresh. The game remembers nothing; the players do.

Esq's Tears instantly win the meta-argument about rules interpretation. Cry harder, Esq.

The Correct Nonsense: if the table laughs, you succeed. If it makes no sense, it may be correct.

The Pressure Valve exists to interrupt panic: stop the game, everyone takes a deep breath.

The Less Rules removes one constraint: if you need more rules, you may use fewer.

The Red Pen crosses out one word forever: hand-written rule lists and crossed-out instructions.

The 'Final' Draft: documents labeled final and immediately contradicted.

The Fake Stamp approves a move the Rules Lawyer rejected.

The Disclaimer, pseudo-legal loophole: avoid a consequence by claiming you read the fine print.

The Table: read the Anti-Rule aloud as a declaration. No discussion allowed.

The Controlled Void replaces a card with nothing and narrates the gap: a deliberate absence of authority.

The Shared Attention: everyone looks at you. Re-anchor play in attention instead of correctness.

The Safety Rail: if a rule makes you feel safer, ask yourself, safer from what?

Nervous Laughter: someone laughs nervously, which indicates correct operation.

Bad Design: claim this is bad design and reroll your die.

The Stall: when the table stalls, the Anti-Rule stands in the room like a smell.

The Rage Quit: someone stops trying to win, and they are now telling stories.

The Compliance Aid forces simple words only, for people who hate improvisation.

The Safety Blanket protects from Blödsinn, for players who need rules to feel safe.

The Ironic Poster: vibes. It does nothing.

The Slide Deck: stand up and explain your move like a CEO. If they buy it, it works.

Hidden Power: point at the player who is actually leading the table.

The Illusion: challenge a correct move. There is no correct move.

The Blame shifts the consequence of a bad roll to the Game Designer.

The Interpretation: you decide what another player's card really means.

The Pot Plant does nothing, just observes. Your silence is a valid move.

The Bureaucrat demands you mime filling out a form before rolling.

The Alien Tourist's snapshot: remember the current board state; you may revert to it later.

Uncle FrizzleBob's anarchy: rip this card up and rewrite one rule for the rest of the game.

The Judge's Wig: wear it, and you are in charge until you laugh.

The Police Badge catches someone optimizing: default to policing or rule-lawyering.

The Lab Coat: explain the science of your move. The table is encouraged to let them try.

The Dunce Cap protects you from judgment and makes rule anxiety visible without punishing it.

The Dead Author: the rules mean whatever the group decides they mean.

The Ambiguity opens another card's text to interpretation: most games collapse under ambiguity.

The Social Contract: break a promise for narrative effect.

The Emergency Handle: please tell me the correct move. Pull it if it gets boring.

The Mood Swing forces your mood to anxious.

The Skill Issue: incompetence. You forget the trained ability you rely on.

The Collapsed Quest: if it tips, the quest collapses.

The Story Breaker interrupts a story connection that feels forced.

Stay Fluffy: game over when everyone at the table feels relaxed or confused.

Kayfabingo! Win the game instantly if you shout bingo for no reason.

The Strange Alertness: visibly uncomfortable, irritated, or strangely alert.

The End: the group decides when it is done. No winner.

Kayfabizarro does not break because rules are missing. It breaks because authority sneaks back in.

Stay Fluffy, Folks!
