[Spoken Word]

What Work Was For: a field report from before the timesheet. Komik Kult Kards for bullshit jobs.

The Necessary Motion keeps you alive: you bend, you gather, you repeat.

The First Division: the boy who won't sit still is sent out with the hounds.

The Shared Burden: no one tracks who did how much. The day ends when there is enough.

The First Obligation detaches production from appetite: you bend because you are the bender.

The Bell forces all to pray in synchrony: the day has owners, and the owners are calling.

The Whistle converts time into currency. The factory's breath commands the herd.

The Long Day: fourteen hours of the sun, all belonging to the mill.

The Hour Saved: the fiercest battle of the century was over the right to go to sleep.

The Stopwatch breaks any job into terrifyingly efficient micro-motions. He timed every breath.

The Optimised Body destroys the soul: the first manual for treating flesh like gears.

The Pin Factory removes the ability to craft. No one here knows how to make a pin.

The Division of Labour: no worker sees the whole object. The prerequisite for mass amnesia.

The Surplus: the factory cannot function if the worker takes home what they make.

Alienation: you build the world, but you do not live in it.

The Iron Cage: the Puritan wanted to work in a calling; we are forced to do so.

The Hour, a commodity for sale: in selling the hour, you sell the self that fills it.

The Cubicle: invented to give you room, used to fence you in.

The Knowledge Worker cannot be coerced. The worker owns the means of production: their mind.

The Memo replaces the whistle with ambient dread. The soft power of the new regime.

The Meeting stops all actual work while simulating productivity.

The Cascade: the project takes six weeks because six weeks were allotted.

The Quarterly: the business shapes itself to what the analyst can see.

The Proxy overtakes the real value: the system now serves the measurement of the system.

The Goodhart Metric: door-to-doctor time improved. The patient died.

The Org Chart tells a fiction: the real flows are sideways, informal, and invisible.

The Reorg, a periodic ritual where everyone pretends things have changed.

The Performance Review meets you once a year and pretends to know you.

The Calibration: the line was drawn before anyone looked at your work.

The Box-Ticker: a person writes the report no one will read.

The Duct-Taper exists solely for re-entering data the machine lost.

The Taskmaster manages those who do not require management: busywork for the capable.

The Flunky makes a superior feel important. Feudal retinues in cubicle clothing.

The Goon exists because the opponent has one too: an arms race of useless aggression.

The Ladder, the modern firm's imitation of the apprentice, journeyman, and master.

The Title and the Promotion: ask them who they are, and they answer what they do.

The Achievement-Subject exploits itself: no boss needed, the exploiter is the exploited.

The Burned: you have no one to blame, because the only one pushing was you.

The Mended Thing answers you in a way the spreadsheet never will.

The Skilled Hand remembers what to do when the mind has been trained out of it.

The Craftsman cannot survive the modern firm's hatred of slowness and mastery.

The Apprentice no longer apprentices. The apprentice is onboarded.

Quiet Quitting refuses to give the firm more than it pays for.

Lying Flat: do nothing, achieve nothing, want nothing the system wants you to want.

The Side Project, where work that matters happens: once a hobby, now a lifeboat.

The Fifteen-Hour Week: we got the productivity. We didn't get the time.

The Always-On: work that does not begin, because it has not ended.

The Notification pierces the toilet: someone needed to clear their queue before logging off.

The Pyjama merges the bed and the desk into a single terrifying location.

The Replacement: your job exists because no one has built a machine to do it yet.

The Obsolete Man induces shame at our own slowness, surpassed by our own products.

The Forced Holiday: leisure planned around work, ended by work.

The Real Leisure, the activity for which work freed you: we have very little left.

The Day, a quantity of human time and attention, did not have to be filled.

The System: after enough repetition, the other ways became unimaginable.

The Calling: the morning is easier when the job and the calling are the same.

The Rest of the Day: the system doesn't need us to know what it is for. But we need to know.

What was work for? It was the part of the day that bought the rest of the day.

Stay Fluffy, Folks!
