What Work Was For: a field report from before the timesheet.

Ever wonder what work was for? It started with The Necessary Motion: you bend, you gather, you repeat.

Back then, The First Division: the boy who won't sit still is sent out with the hounds.

And nobody counted. The Shared Burden: no one tracks who did how much. The day ends when there is enough.

Until The First Obligation detaches production from appetite: you bend because you are the bender.

Hear that? That's The Bell: the day has owners, and the owners are calling.

Later it got louder: The Whistle converts time into currency you spend. The factory's breath commands the herd.

How long? Ask The Long Day: fourteen hours of the sun, all belonging to the mill.

So people fought for The Hour Saved: the fiercest battle of the century was over the right to go to sleep.

Meanwhile a man with a clipboard invented The Stopwatch, breaking any job into terrifyingly efficient micro-motions. He timed every breath.

His masterpiece was The Optimised Body, the first manual for treating flesh like gears.

Ask anyone in The Pin Factory: no one here knows how to make a pin.

That's the point of The Division of Labour: no worker sees the whole object.

Where does the rest go? Into The Surplus: the factory cannot function if the worker takes home what they make.

Marx had a word for it: Alienation. You build the world, but you do not live in it.

Weber called it The Iron Cage: the Puritan wanted to work in a calling; we are forced to do so.

Did you know The Cubicle was invented to give you room? Used to fence you in.

Inside sits The Knowledge Worker, who cannot be coerced; must be manipulated and persuaded.

How? First with The Memo, replacing the factory whistle with ambient dread.

Then with The Meeting, which stops all actual work while simulating productivity.

Why six weeks? Ask The Cascade: the project takes six weeks because six weeks were allotted.

Upstairs they live by The Quarterly: the business shapes itself to what the analyst can see.

My favourite statistic is The Goodhart Metric: door-to-doctor time improved. The patient died.

Who decides? Look at The Org Chart, a fiction about how decisions flow.

When that fails, there's The Reorg, a periodic ritual where everyone pretends things have changed.

Once a year you get The Performance Review, where the organization meets you and pretends to know you.

Spoiler: it was settled by The Calibration. The line was drawn before anyone looked at your work.

Graeber counted five kinds of useless job. Meet The Box-Ticker: a person writes the report no one will read.

Next to him sits The Duct-Taper, re-entering data the machine lost.

Their boss is The Taskmaster, creating busywork for the capable.

His boss keeps The Flunky, to make a superior feel important.

And why hire The Goon? Because the opponent has one too. An arms race of useless aggression.

At parties you meet The Title and the Promotion: you ask them who they are, and they answer what they do.

Who needs a boss when you have The Achievement-Subject? The exploiter is at the same time the exploited.

Which ends with The Burned: you have no one to blame, because the only one pushing was you.

Try fixing a chair. The Mended Thing answers you in a way the spreadsheet never will.

My grandfather was The Craftsman: cannot survive the modern firm's hatred of slowness and mastery.

His grandson got The Apprentice: the apprentice no longer apprentices. The apprentice is onboarded.

So he does Quiet Quitting: refuses to give the firm more than it pays for.

In China they call it Lying Flat: do nothing, achieve nothing, want nothing the system wants you to want.

His real work happens in The Side Project: once a hobby, now a lifeboat.

Keynes promised us The Fifteen-Hour Week. We got the productivity. We didn't get the time.

Instead we got The Always-On: work that does not begin, because it has not ended.

Even on the toilet: The Notification. Someone needed to clear their queue before logging off.

Working from home means The Pyjama merges the bed and the desk into a single terrifying location.

Job security? Ask The Replacement: your job exists because no one has built a machine to do it yet.

Need a break? Book The Forced Holiday: leisure planned around work, ended by work.

Pieper wanted The Real Leisure: the activity for which work freed you. We have very little left.

Here's the twist: The Day did not have to be filled.

We filled it anyway, with The System: after enough repetition, the other ways became unimaginable.

Marcus Aurelius would add: The Calling. The morning is easier when the job and the calling are the same.

So what was it all for? The Rest of the Day: the point of it all.

What was work for? It was the part of the day that bought the rest of the day.
