Mind the wheelbarrow. If you came in through the gate, grab a trowel. If nobody came, I'll talk to the lettuce. It listens better than the news.

Out on the street, every living room has two armchairs, one says doom, one says saved. Mine went on the compost. Some call what's left protopia. I call it the next small job.

The beaver, who builds his dam one stick a night, has drawn the whole future on one page and taps it: the future is a process of slow, incremental improvement you participate in.

So the raccoon steals the eraser, rubs out half of it, and grins: draw the next step in pencil. Pencil means you expect to be wrong and stay anyway.

Which offends the possum, who plays dead at every meeting, then comes back to life with his crossed-out map: make the first version bad on purpose. Bad is how you know it is real.

Above them the woodpecker hammers the same hole in the plum tree, ninth spring running: Do not finish it. Improve it once, then leave it better and open.

So we stop drawing and fix something.

The iguana stays cold-blooded while the city smokes behind him and oils my gate: one broken thing close enough to touch.

Not world peace. A hinge. A repair you can finish this week, not a revolution you can only announce.

The turtle carries her house on her back and never hurries; she trims the fence in the rain: small fixes do not trend. They just hold.

The badger, who digs the same tunnel every year, lays a checkmark on a tile, then another: One repair a week is fifty-two a year.

But the radio starts shouting. The sloth in the apple tree, slowest animal alive, breathes out slow and blue: take the one breath the feed is built to deny you.

Next door the boar, who roots up every garden on the street, has also fixed every garden on the street: Authority is overrated, proximity is underrated. Ask the one who actually does the thing.

He sends me to the capybara on the bench, friends with every species, interviewed by none: real know-how is often badly dressed and lives three doors down.

The pelican, who will carry anything in his beak, carries a staple gun to my shed: Turn watching into showing up. Name a time, a place, a task. Saturday, nine. Bring gloves.

So on Saturday: A pigeon, an emu, a duck and a crow huddled over a coffee carafe: the first small, clumsy gathering. Clumsy is the entry fee. Four birds, and not one of them flies in formation.

Then the octopus turns up late, one hammer in every arm, and we split a problem the way your ancestors raised a barn.

The hedgehogs at the hedge keep their spines up, until one lowers them for a tiny hi. Lower the bar to contact. Most cooperation starts embarrassingly small.

The quokka, who smiles at everyone, rings the big bell anyway, and nobody comes: they might not respond. Your nervous system thinks that is death. It is not.

At dusk the bear, who sleeps through every news cycle, writes in his notebook with yesterday's papers in the compost: Write down what you actually saw, not what the headline said you would see.

The city pigeon walks the crossings all day, so she paints one tiny stripe on the worst one: Run a tiny version. Low stakes, real feedback, no permission needed.

And the crow, who never forgets a face, fixes the panel marked do not repair while the guard looks away: improvement often looks like mild disobedience to a stuck system.

The toad sits between the two armchairs under a question-mark umbrella, getting rained on, smiling. Toads like rain: trade the warm baths of hopeless and handled for the cold shower of it depends on us.

The camel, who stores water for the long road, lays one brick for every week on the calendar: there is no final version. The cost is showing up again next week.

And the mole digs on in the dark, where no one could clap even if they wanted to: Nobody will clap. Start anyway. The clapping was never the point.

Then a golden paw hands me a pen: Forget the brochure. Ignore the loop. Pick up the pencil.

Doom gets the subscriptions. Rescue gets the tickets. A garden with a squeaky gate earns nothing, which is how you know it's yours.

If you're still at the gate, gloves are on the wheelbarrow. If you're not, the lettuce and I start without you.
