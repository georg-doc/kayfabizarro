[Spoken Word]

Mind the wheelbarrow. If you came in through the gate, grab a trowel. If nobody came, I'll talk to the lettuce. It listens better than the news.

Out on the street, every living room has two armchairs, one says doom, one says saved. Mine went on the compost. Some call what's left protopia. I call it the next small job.

The beaver on the fence post taps his pencil: the future is a process of slow, incremental improvement you participate in.

The raccoon rubs out half my plan and grins: draw the next step in pencil. Pencil means you expect to be wrong and stay anyway.

The possum holds up his crossed-out map, proud as a king: make the first version bad on purpose. Bad is how you know it is real.

The woodpecker drills one word into the plum tree, again: Improve it once, then leave it better and open.

The iguana in the tool belt oils my hinge while the city smokes behind him: one broken thing close enough to touch.

Not world peace. A hinge. A repair you can finish this week, not a revolution you can only announce.

The turtle in the raincoat trims the fence, unbothered: small fixes do not trend. They just hold.

The badger lays a checkmark on a tile, then another: One repair a week is fifty-two a year. Nobody clapped, and the place is different.

When the radio starts shouting, the sloth in the apple tree breathes out slow and blue: take the one breath the feed is built to deny you.

Next door, the boar with the wrench: Authority is overrated, proximity is underrated. Ask the one who actually does the thing.

He means the capybara on the bench, badly dressed, never interviewed: real know-how is often badly dressed and lives three doors down.

The pelican staples a flyer to my shed: Turn watching into showing up. Name a time, a place, a task. Saturday, nine. Bring gloves.

And who turns up? A pigeon, an emu, a duck and a crow huddled over a coffee carafe: the first small, clumsy gathering. Clumsy is the entry fee.

The octopus takes the shed roof, all eight arms at once: split a problem the way your ancestors raised a barn.

Two hedgehogs by the hedge, one manages a tiny hi. Lower the bar to contact. Most cooperation starts embarrassingly small.

The quokka rings the big bell alone and nobody comes: they might not respond. Your nervous system thinks that is death. It is not.

The grumpy bear in glasses writes in his notebook, newspapers in the compost: Write down what you actually saw, not what the headline said you would see.

The city pigeon with a paint roller leaves one tiny stripe on the crossing: Run a tiny version. Low stakes, real feedback, no permission needed.

The crow in the tactical vest fixes the panel marked do not repair: improvement often looks like mild disobedience to a stuck system.

The toad under the question-mark umbrella still can't pick an armchair. The Comfort of Maybe: trade the warm baths of hopeless and handled for the cold shower of it depends on us.

The camel lays one brick for every week on the calendar: there is no final version. The cost is showing up again next week.

And the mole digs on with no one watching: Nobody will clap. Start anyway. The clapping was never the point.

Then a golden paw hands me a pen: Forget the brochure. Ignore the loop. Pick up the pencil.

Doom gets the subscriptions. Rescue gets the tickets. A garden with a squeaky gate earns nothing, which is how you know it's yours.

If you're still at the gate, gloves are on the wheelbarrow. If you're not, the lettuce and I start without you.
