[Spoken Word]

Big Bad Brain Wrestling: every move a lie, every bruise for real.

The kid who owns me got me for his birthday: four heads, one body, a cardboard ring.

First he clicks on the golden head: The Babyface, high-flying moves; audiences cheer, a slave to approval.

His sister yawns, so he clicks the sneer: The Heel, eye pokes; if they aren't booing, you aren't doing your job.

Now she cheers for the wrong one: The Tweener, you don't ask for it, so they want you more.

Somebody has to lose. Guess which head: The Jobber, isolated and picked apart; you exist to make the other guy look good.

Losing hurts, so I learn to fake it: The Master Seller, reel back, limp to the ring, make them believe.

Faking needs noise: The Thigh Slap, a loud sound suggests high impact; an audio lie.

Mom bans slapping, so we stomp: The Stomp Punch, kinetic misdirection.

The sister tries it and lands in the fish tank: The Oversell Clown, flying backwards from a gentle tap.

Meanwhile the kid writes every ending on the cereal box: The Booker, spider in suit; the ending is pre-planned, you are just a puppet.

He invites the whole class. Nobody comes: The Empty Arena, pausing for a pop that never comes.

So he records the dog barking and plays it back: The Green Screen Crowd, audio sweetening on a lie.

His cousin reviews the show online, never once picked me up: The Internet Smark, psychology without ever taking a bump.

But the dog wants a real match. One bite, no arm: The Real Injury, unplanned; the crowd goes nuts because it's real.

Mom says we never speak of the arm: The Kayfabe Police, keep the secrets secret, or get fired.

One arm left, so the kid books the big finish: The Finisher, the Leg Drop; if this hits, go home.

It hits. The sister counts very slowly: The False Finish, the art of the near-fall.

At Christmas a new figure arrives: The Retirement Match, rising, and falling; see you next season.

Back in the drawer: four heads, one arm. Every bruise for real.

Stay Fluffy, Folks!
