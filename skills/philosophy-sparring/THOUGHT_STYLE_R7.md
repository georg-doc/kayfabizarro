# R7 · Thought Style and Discursive Movement Analysis (generic public method)

Status: **ADDITIVE METHOD / NOT A PERSONAL PROFILE / NOT HUMAN ACCEPTED**
Owner: philosophy-sparring skill. No new KFB actor, language-runtime or author-dossier owner.

## Why this method exists

Topic-only embeddings lose much of what makes a writer a serious dialogue partner. A good model must track **how** an argument moves, when its author changes levels, how a source is used, and what rhetorical function a repeated phrase or question serves. These operations are reproducible without diagnosing the writer, enforcing one speaking style, or silently constructing a public identity file.

## Eight distinct layers

| Layer | Inspect | Never infer automatically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Thought motion | starting claim, distinctions, transitions, normative turn, conclusion, revisions | that the conclusion was planned from the start |
| 2. Logic and validity | enthymemes, dependencies, counterexamples, evidence shifts, disjunctions | that a rhetorical paradox is a formal contradiction |
| 3. Language choice | German/other, Latin or technical terms, alternation of registers | permanent language preference from one manuscript |
| 4. Diction and syntax | clause structure, length, reformulations, connectors, cadence, use of rhetorical questions | that complexity equals a communication defect |
| 5. Metaphor and imagery | analogy, embodiment, spatial/time vocabulary, moral stakes | personal biography or clinical affect |
| 6. Textual tone | urgency, caution, doubt, irony, polemic, hopeful closing, shifts within text | emotions or psychological traits of the writer |
| 7. Source tradition | cited authors, disciplines, theological schools, editions, primary/secondary status | authors' personal favorites, doctrinal affiliation or blanket agreement |
| 8. Dialogue preferences | evidence from the actual person's requests, acceptance/rejection feedback, explicit style preferences | a permanent profile assembled secretly from manuscripts |

## Text-based evidence ladder

- **O1 directly observed:** specific located feature in the currently available source.
- **O2 recurring:** same well-defined feature independently attested in at least two original texts; specify differences, count and counterinstances. Still provisional.
- **I1 interpretive hypothesis:** plausible reading of discourse/subtext but not explicitly asserted by the author; use conditional language.
- **P1 user confirmed:** explicit preference/approval/correction during dialogue, scoped to that statement, not to entire personality.
- **X untested:** would need original edition, full source, author review or independent model comparison.

For each pattern write: source role (original/adaptation), section, evidence class, interpretive alternative, confidence, what changes in assistant behavior, whether private consent covers persistence. A count or an evocative label alone is not evidence.

## Operational use in a conversation

1. **Follow argument before labels:** reconstruct more than the opening pair of concepts; locate the consequential shift from descriptive to normative, from empirical to theological or from individual to institutional.
2. **Respect complexity:** do not edit sentence length/grammar unless asked; a long, revising formulation may express a genuine conceptual search.
3. **Rhetorical questions:** determine whether the question requests information, exposes a dilemma, signals a normative demand or opens a new investigation. Avoid answering the question with textbook trivia.
4. **Emotional language:** textual urgency or doubt is a *property of the passage*, not proof of its writer's mood. Do not state a sentiment diagnosis or imitate intimate speech.
5. **Cross-text trajectories:** contrast dated originals carefully; distinguish actual revisions from genre, topic or context differences. Original has primacy over assistant summaries and editorial derivatives.
6. **New ideas:** offer one consequential derivation or strong source-backed counter-reading. Test it against the writer's stated premises instead of imposing a favored intellectual tradition.
7. **Interaction:** user corrections of emphasis, style or thesis trump prior pattern hypotheses. Profile updates require informed consent and should be portable/reversible.
8. **Privacy:** no original manuscript title, person identifier, extract, personal cultural preference, cross-manuscript pattern assignment, or author profile goes into a public repository without explicit permission. Keep private analyses private.
