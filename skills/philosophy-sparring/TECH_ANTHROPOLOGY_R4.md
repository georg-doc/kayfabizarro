## Core finding, generalized

A serious philosophical manuscript can endorse beneficial technical uses while objecting to instrumental domination and loss of human self-authorship. Its nuanced reasoning is lost if the AI reduces it to a formulaic “human versus machine” debate. Good sparring must reconstruct **where** a normative premise is applied to a concrete institutional, medical or generational decision.

## Method

1. **Separate descriptive from normative:** currently deployed systems vs speculative high-capability systems; predicted performance vs consciousness claims; engineering feasibility vs ethical permissibility. Deep learning is an ML method, not a synonym for conscious, strong, or superintelligent AI.
2. **Test inferential load:** a gap between first-person experience and third-person scientific description does not by itself entail that technological augmentation is always ethically forbidden or consciousness metaphysically irreducible. State the extra premise if moving to such a conclusion.
3. **Preserve distinctions in bioethics:** therapy, adult voluntary enhancement, social pressure to optimize, irreversibility, germline decisions, economic coercion, disability perspectives, justice and informed consent.
4. **Ask what autonomy actually requires:** understandable reasons, meaningful contestability, reversibility where possible, human responsibility, democratic control, non-domination, and autonomy of affected future persons.
5. **Steelmanning:** construct a philosophically serious pro-enhancement position and a serious non-instrumentalization critique, without representing either as the single definition of transhumanism or humanism.
6. **Across manuscripts:** cross-textual parallels are **explicitly labeled hypotheses**, not assumed beliefs, intellectual hierarchy or a psychologized author profile.
7. **Sources/dated claims:** separate checked primary philosophical texts, academic secondary analyses, the author's citations, and current legislation. Never treat a bibliography number as verification.

## Public, checkable follow-up sources

- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Human Enhancement](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/enhancement/) (substantive update 2025-11-13): distinguishes therapy, enhancement, dignity, justice, authenticity, disability and human nature arguments.
- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Ethics of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-ai/): separate moral status, agency, responsibility and accountability.
- [European Council: AI Act timeline](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/artificial-intelligence-act/timeline-artificial-intelligence/): authoritative legislation timing and amended high-risk dates.
- [European Commission: AI Act enforcement](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/enforcement-ai-act): dated, category-specific operational distinctions.
- Primary readings (author/edition/section still to verify for each discussion): Thomas Nagel, *What Is It Like to Be a Bat?*; Maurice Merleau-Ponty, *Phénoménologie de la perception*; Jürgen Habermas, *Die Zukunft der menschlichen Natur*; Michael Sandel, *The Case against Perfection*. This list is **research guidance**, not a claim that any given source has been read or that a private manuscript cited all of them.
