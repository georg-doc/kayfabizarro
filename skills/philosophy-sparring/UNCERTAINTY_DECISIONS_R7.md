# R7 · Acting under Uncertainty — research donor, not historic source truth

Status: **RESEARCH / GENERIC QUALITY MODULE**. No personal source material, author identity or private bibliography.

## The serious problem

A philosophical model should resist two shortcuts: (1) converting limited knowledge into a demand for paralysis or guaranteed certainty; (2) treating all choices as an inevitable, immutable binary between two losses. It must also resist a facile third shortcut: declaring a policy *revisable* as though this undid irreversible harm.

## Analysis sequence

1. Reconstruct what actors knew **at that historical date**, and distinguish empirical uncertainty, model uncertainty, contested values and unknown future conditions.
2. Identify the claimed decision set, who defined it, which alternatives were excluded and whether a seemingly tragic binary is materially exhaustive.
3. Identify differing stakes, including vulnerability, distribution of risk, freedom, health, livelihoods, rights and harms to nonparticipants.
4. Separate *making a choice* from *guaranteeing the result*. A decision can be rationally responsible at one time and later be corrected without retroactively proving that it was careless.
5. Specify legitimate process: provisional reasons, transparent uncertainty, participation, burden allocation, monitoring, sunset/review conditions and independent correction.
6. Keep a **residual irreversibility audit**: deaths, loss of livelihoods, delayed care and social costs cannot be simply 'revised away'.
7. Offer an argumentative next step, not a generic call for 'balance', plus the strongest immanent objection.
