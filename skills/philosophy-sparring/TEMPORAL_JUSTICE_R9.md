## Purpose

Intellectual dialogue should not collapse different concepts of improvement into a single supposed solution. Identical words used in theology, political emancipation and technology may carry distinct agents, commitments, beneficiaries and time horizons. A genuinely strong comparison identifies both the **limited analogy** and the point at which it stops supporting a conclusion.

## Comparison framework

| Dimension | Research question |
| --- | --- |
| Agent | Who is expected to act, and with what legitimate authority? |
| Beneficiary | Whose vulnerability, opportunity or injury is at stake? |
| Time direction | Past irreparable suffering, current injustice, future possibility? |
| End state | Incremental repair, emancipation, final reconciliation or full salvation? |
| Moral premise | A publicly reasoned obligation, a technical optimization criterion, a faith commitment? |
| Reversibility | What can be amended, compensated, prevented or **not** undone? |
| Evidence | Exact historical work, quoted edition, wider text context, exegesis status? |
| Counterexample | Does another movement use similar words but reject the alleged end goal? |

## The consequential distinction

- Reducing future harms does not physically undo harms already done to people who will never receive the benefits.
- Institutions can pursue truth, reparation, acknowledgment, non-repetition and the rights of descendants, while recognizing limits of restorative power.
- A theological promise of ultimate justice is distinct from an empirical claim that historical injuries have been reversed; neither may be smuggled into a policy analysis without its premises.
- Criticism of totalizing perfection does not entail rejecting limited, fallible medical, legal or social improvement.

## Dialog quality bar

Avoid generic ‘religious vs secular?’ dialectic. Trace a concrete argument: if a project claims to emancipate or improve persons, what does it owe to those excluded from future gains? Formulate one consequential implication, one serious counterposition and the additional premise needed to choose between them. Do not read rhetorical urgency as author temperament or make this newest motif the default for an unrelated text.
