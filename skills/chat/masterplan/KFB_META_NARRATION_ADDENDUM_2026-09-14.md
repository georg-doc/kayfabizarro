## Lead assessment

### 1. Closure is the strongest cross-module grammar in this package

The collection does more than repeat McCloud terminology. It shows one recurring mechanic across several scales:

- semantic gaps between cards;
- physical gutters;
- portal holes;
- missing words in Fluff-o-lect;
- a `Sell it` interpretation that may be rejected;
- opaque gateways that deliberately withhold the destination.

This is useful because it connects narrative form, spatial interaction and player choice without requiring a universal runtime system.

Direction: treat **closure as a design grammar**, not automatically as a new engine module.

### 2. Town aligns strongly with `world as toy` and embodied state

Two Lab principles are particularly compatible with the current Town North Star:

- affordance before instruction: the shape of an object suggests the action;
- state is embodied rather than shown in a detached UI.

This reinforces existing Town directions such as game-piece architecture, embodied tools, the card fan instead of a numeric collection counter, and NPC/world reactions instead of abstract relationship meters.

No new Town `J-` decision is created by this observation.

### 3. `Show it · Spin it · Sell it` is a useful encounter grammar

The Lab collection describes a three-beat progression:

1. show/describe before judging;
2. spin from a character/register;
3. sell an interpretation as an offer rather than a forced conclusion.

This could become a useful donor grammar for Presenter/Podcast, Town encounters, card viewing and guided museum content.

Boundary: consumers may adopt it, but it is not yet a mandatory event API.

### 4. Triplets fit Town, but the middle relation is the important part

The collection sharpens the existing Triplet idea: the semantic leverage often sits in the relation/middle phrase rather than in the nouns. That is potentially useful for generating compact NPC lines without turning Town into a generic joke machine.

Town should continue to reuse the existing ChatterBox/Triplet lineage rather than create a second dialogue grammar.

### 5. The recurring `six` pattern is interesting, not yet a hard invariant

The source observes sixes across story modes, transitions, zones, table seats, connectors and a 16:9-derived card horizon.

Treat this as an **observed structural resonance**. Do not force new systems to contain six items merely to preserve the pattern. A pattern discovered from independent constraints is valuable; a pattern imposed afterward becomes decoration.

### 6. `Build` and `judge` separation belongs in production method

The collection reports the same principle emerging independently through blind reading, critics without code access and model self-bias: the builder should not be the sole judge of its own result.

This is broader than Town. It should inform QA/review workflow and may later be folded into the provider-neutral production SOP after concrete wording is agreed.

Do not treat this addendum as that SOP change by itself.

### 7. The six open tensions should stay open

Especially important:

- **Profiling vs satire:** diegetic presentation may make profiling visible, but it does not remove the fact that data exists. Any actual implementation needs an explicit data/privacy contract; diegesis is presentation, not a privacy guarantee.
- **Collection visibility vs counting:** a growing physical fan can communicate accumulation without turning the experience into completion pressure, but the tension remains intentional.
- **Randomness vs meaning:** procedural connections cannot carry stronger factual claims than their generation rules support.
- **Emergence vs readability:** combinations are only valuable when the player can still attribute cause and effect.

Do not resolve these by documentation fiat.

## Meta-canon consolidation direction

WS0's `KFB_META_COMPENDIUM_v1.md` reports repeated editable copies of Layer Zero, Global Intent, Infinite Comic and Almanac material.

Recommended next step is **not a bulk file move**.

First create a small `CANON_HOME_MAP` that records, per concept/document:

- proposed canonical home;
- published surface where relevant;
- known duplicate locations;
- whether each duplicate is a pointer, snapshot or still-editable fork;
- verification status.

Only after the true home is verified should touched duplicates become pointers or `ARCHIVED HISTORY`. Do not delete historical copies merely to reduce count.

This is `reuse before rebuild` applied to concepts.
