## Observed mechanic (not a claim about ancient calendars)

- Main game board contains one **26-tooth Tzolk’in gear** connected to five other location gears.
- Five receiver locations: **Palenque, Uxmal, Tikal, Yaxchilan, Chichen Itza**. Their distinct action offerings are controlled by the published game.
- After player turns, the central gear usually advances one tooth; its connected gears carry placed workers to progressively different action locations.
- During a player's turn, they normally must **either place workers OR retrieve some/all workers**, not both.
- Placing workers costs corn; retrieving triggers actions at their attained positions. Holding a worker longer typically increases the value of actions but postpones access; in the original game, a worker may also reach an invalid end point.
- Four food/scoring checkpoints and a terminal central-wheel revolution create long-term pacing.
- **Critical separation:** the **26-tooth central game-wheel** is a deliberate physical tabletop abstraction and is **not** the historical 260-day Tzolk’in count. The 13×20 calendar pair remains separate arithmetic.

## Why it helps KFB: mechanics, not visual cloning

The strong donor is the **three-way temporal choice**:

1. **Place / invest** — choose a real scene socket or Almanac/prop link and lodge a small named intent (not a generic “worker” clone).
2. **Wait / mature** — a persistent, visible world/scene event moves the intent forward through predefined states. Waiting may improve utility, reveal a new story relationship, or increase risk.
3. **Claim / activate** — retrieve at a deliberately chosen time, causing **one existing-owner action**. Early retrieval can be useful and is not uniformly wrong.

The game transforms time from a passive number into a **visible opportunity-cost machine**. KFB does not need corn economics, rival workers, temple scores, unique Mayan places, the exact six-gear layout or the game's winning conditions.

## Original KFB design grammar (PROPOSAL, not frozen)

Working title: **ChronoSocket / Time Loom Diorama**.

- **ChronoLoom core** already owns only read-only 260-state indexing.
- **One existing world object/diorama** offers 1–3 *story sockets* belonging to that owner; tokens are small visual attachments to real KFB source props (not a new global owner).
- **Timeline exposure:** a source-authored kinetic circular/spiral *prop* may rotate when the owning game's story tick changes. It must not silently advance world time per animation frame.
- **Wait-to-mature rule:** per socket, an intent has start tick and authored stages (e.g. Dawn → Growth → Fluff Resonance → Disclosure). The 13 video-tone phases can optionally describe that process, but the prop may use only 3–5 salient thresholds in first proof.
- **Claim:** player collects a *real* card/Almanac memory, changes a prop state, unlocks an existing portal destination or starts a defined VFX cue. Never automatically instantiate a nonexistent memory.
- **Coherence:** deterministic seed and source object ID; actual state kept in the authoritative store. Visual rings/gear rotation **read** the same state. On save/fresh reload, the outcome does not jump.
- **Fractal zoom:** repeated authoring pattern at prop, diorama, card and memory levels; not separate nested 260-day runtimes.
- **Clarity:** the player can infer **what is maturing, when it can be claimed, and the cost of waiting**. Avoid generic clock chrome; use approved clay/cut-paper KFB object vocabulary.

## First design comparison (one bounded gate, after ChronoGlyph look source-isolation)

Use one KFB-source-backed diorama with two differently themed props. Provide three alternative *original* visual readings of the same “invest, wait, claim” contract:
- **Orbital Time-Knot:** a compact asymmetrical rotating object with two visible attachment notches.
- **Breathing Spiral:** deformation/pulse rather than mechanical cogs; possible source-backed magnetic ribbon across anchors.
- **Toy-Machine Gear (authored):** a distinct, wonky clay gear/ratchet prop whose working principle is legible but whose silhouette/placement does not reproduce CGE's published board.

For each compare: immediate vs delayed claim, maturing state readability, stationary camera, Day/Golden Hour/Graveyard Night, no extra overlay HUD, stable source objects and persistence. The actual ChronoGlyph style comparison remains the **one current gate**; this board-game receiver comparison is downstream and deferred until it passes.
