## 1. Central design thesis
**The tutorial is the story, and the story is a sequence of things the player learns to do.**
FrizzleBob begins as a *figure trapped in the story* rather than a camera looking at a tutorial. His first independent action produces his first gap. He discovers that he can cross that gap, handle a sourced Card, hear incompatible interpretations, remember an encounter, move through the landscape, and choose where to go. The objective is not to solve a doctrine or fill an XP meter; the player constructs provisional **closure** from partial evidence, then can revise it.

Avoid the clichéd amnesia-as-reset trap: the player and FrizzleBob lack *opening context*, but the world's past events and other NPC memories are not cleared. A later save/reload must preserve player-made traces. NPC knowledge varies; the Lorekeeper does not possess an omniscient manual.

### Four experiential promises, taught by doing
1. **Physical comic world:** the frame, gutter, missing panel, paper edge, ink mark, Card and tangible scene have spatial consequences.
2. **Agency and response:** observe -> touch/take/compare -> world reacts -> remember or reject; even a wrong guess has a legible outcome.
3. **Plural accounts:** Clown/Resident/Lorekeeper disagree without one labeling the player correct; **SHOW IT -> SPIN IT -> SELL IT** is witnessed, not lectured.
4. **Connected toy modes:** walk/jump and inspect first, early vehicle/Drive second, other authentic owner-backed portals later; never promise unimplemented Flight/Combat buttons.

This is NOT a hard checklist gating exploration. If the user skips a beat, diegetic affordances stay discoverable on re-entry.

## 3. Cinematic spatial transitions — without false loading portals

**A. Panel -> Gutter:** show the original comic frame from *inside*, then let the player physically cross its thickness. The panel backdrop/ink may be a flat illustrated layer; the player's contact and world coordinates must remain owned by the existing World/Surface/Camera runtime. Avoid secretly swapping player controllers at the threshold.

**B. Gutter -> Terrain:** a real topological change in what is visible and traversable. Contrasting negative space, inked edges and an authentic 3D clay/paper landscape supply the revelation. No generic level lobby. The image may change from constrained panel composition to free camera, but the controller/camera truth has one owner.

**C. Terrain -> Town:** place coherent social reasons before decorations: the Driver is at a road, the Clown near gathering space, the Lorekeeper near a book/card study. The Castle/tower serves distant orientation. This specifically repairs the known R4 problem of objects at coordinates lacking a network of reasons.

**D. Town -> open regions:** do not secretly recanonize the older six-radial-2D-biome map. The active recovered four-island story has **Town as historical hub** and **Dystopia, Utopia, Protopia** as future lenses, all explorable without a morality quiz. Later procedural Mindscapes/Egregores may affect travel as authored/verified encounters, not replace these regions.

## 4. Optional A/B for the very first 90 seconds

**A — The Torn Panel (preferred for cheap source-faithful proof).** The player walks around three believable props within a 2.5D enclosed vignette, discovers one missing panel edge, jumps over the seam into a 3D Gutter strip. Very low rules burden; strongest immediate show-don't-tell.

**B — The Mined Grid.** The opening room is an illustrated Minesweeper-like micro-board with some unreliable ink glyphs, paper mines and tracks; one safe inference reveals/loosens a frame. Bad choices produce a squash/ink-flare and recovery, never lethal tutorial death. Optional only: do **not** introduce a complete numeric Minesweeper rule set, real punishment or a competing puzzle runtime just to motivate the opening. Needs separate observed fun/clarity proof.

In both, a *single clue* should be on the threshold, for example a matching torn edge on a Card. A player must be able to walk away without having to decode narration.

## 5. Provisional micro-dialogue beats (English / non-canon)
These are **stage cues only**, not new authored character lines or a new NPC script owner. Two short sentences maximum per speech turn, no generic exposition.

- **Opening animated lettering:** “It was a dark and stormy night...” [the final dots visibly resist the frame].
- **Clown on the road:** “There used to be a panel between those two.” [points toward the cut].
- **Resident encountering the Card:** “It says you were expected.” [a second observer disagrees: “It says someone wanted you here.”]
- **Lorekeeper:** “I can show you what remains. I can't tell you who removed it.”
- **Driver:** “Key's by the wheel. Road's yours if you can keep it.”

All lines are editable samples; verify authentic ChatterBox / Resident voice profiles before authored adoption. FrizzleBob's inner commentary, if any, should be sparse and observational, not a quest manager.

## 6. What becomes the ongoing game loop?
**Notice anomaly / landmark -> physically encounter a Card, prop or Resident -> hear or infer a reading -> choose, test or decline -> scene reacts -> provenance/Lean Memory records the event -> a new destination or counterexample appears.**

Depth without grind:
- **Cards are evidence, not XP tokens.** A Card's printed source is unchanged even when participants disagree about its meaning.
- **Closure is the player's act.** For a first player interaction offer two or three curated *connections* rather than asking them to write a thesis.
- **Responses are collectible.** In Town canon (J-14), heard retorts can become later available reply options. Don't create a second inventory; route through existing evidence/Almanac/semantic phrase owners.
- **Objects have consequences.** The same mundane prop can be read, carried, tossed or repurposed only if the receiving physics/interaction owner can actually support those verbs.
- **Memory belongs to the living world.** One Resident may recognize the Card but misunderstand its source; another may recall a prior exchange. NPCs have different partial information.
- **Fluff/Mindscapes** may become resonant world consequences downstream. Do not require a morphic-field engine in this introductory vertical slice.

## 8. Player readability and failure recovery
- The first 30 seconds teach exactly **move/look**, first 90 seconds **one interact**. Reuse the binding Ground controls: W/S forward/back, A/D turn, Q/E strafe, Shift run, Space jump, RMB orbit, wheel zoom (touch/controller adaptation by existing input owner).
- Pointing gestures, a moving NPC, a paper edge, a lamp, and a real road are preferred to giant arrows, minimap markers, popup tips and narrated button lists. One optional localized control hint is permissible where input is genuinely opaque.
- Avoid dark-on-dark usability. The room can *feel* dark but affordances and body silhouette require readable light; ChatterBox subtitles remain readable while voice is unavailable or muted.
- No lethal/looping tutorial punishment; falling through a fake gutter or crashing the car restores the last safe point with persistent discoveries intact. The world may parody a mistake, never destroy user progress.
- Unskippable cinematics at game start max a few seconds. Re-entry, restart, browser focus-loss and reload retain a stable playable state.
- Town's speech budget is **one NPC bubble normal, two simultaneous soft maximum**; roadside media is not counted unless it becomes an actual dialogue bubble.

- **A visible route and early existing sedan key/entry/exit**, integrated only in the actual receiving World/Drive owner with true Track/terrain contact.
- **Open movement after this** rather than a compulsory tutorial lock; at least two meaningful destinations.
- Show one unedited source view and one composition view, short in-browser play capture, exact build/revision; validate restart/reload and recoverable mistakes.

Does the world act like a place with reasons, not tutorial mannequins?
