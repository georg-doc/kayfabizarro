## Required real 3D geometry
- Start with either a verifiably authentic 3D source scan (proven donor) or a transparently labelled physically plausible procedural engineering shell, never a fake screenshot.
- Actual three-dimensional triangulated **inner AND outer bronze surfaces, variable wall/rib profile, thick sound bow/strike ring, open mouth, crown/pivot support, clapper and strike contact point**. Show one original source bell ALONE and prove its GLB/mesh before any scene layout. Separate the physical audio recording identity, 3D identity and measured mode identity.
- Four useful *inspection views* of the SAME object, not generic GUI icons: opaque original solid; real clipped sectional cut showing shell thickness and clapper; X-ray/ghosted surface for interior/exterior correspondence; selected normal-mode displacement with nodes and antinodes **on actual mesh vertices**, optionally exaggerated in slow motion. Bronze becomes optically translucent only as an explanatory X-ray rendering, not a physically transmissive metal.
- Mathematical source truth: microphone FFT gives frequencies/tails but **does not identify the three-dimensional eigenmode displacement field uniquely**. To attach physical mode shape φ_k(v) at each mesh vertex, obtain FEM from real/verified source geometry, material properties (density, E, Poisson), crown mounting constraints, contact force and/or measured holographic modal dataset. Distinguish literature-derived example, analytic illustrative deformation, FE simulated field, and empirically validated field.
- Display scale and time explicitly: bell vibration amplitudes are tiny in physical terms and need **labelled displacement amplification and/or time slow-down**. 48/60 fps directly displaying 200 Hz surface motion aliases; render analytical phase/stroboscopic slow motion or modal envelope instead. Do not map FFT bands to arbitrary equal-radius rings.
- A vibration node is a zero/small displacement region of a mode, antinode a strong one. Map actual φ_k(v) and q_k(t) to surface shading/displacement (u(v,t)=Σ_k q_k(t) φ_k(v)). For sound radiation to water/architecture/terrain, use a separate justified acoustic propagation approximation; do not conflate surface nodes with free-air spherical ripples, nor with EEG/Schumann.
- Audibly preserve dry original bell before any musical pad, creative DSP, bowl, binaural or Egregore interpretation. Any phantom/occult overlays must be an explicitly separate narrative visualization.

## Scientific research grounding
- Perrin/Charnley/dePont 1983 experimental nodal partials & FEM (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022460X83904017). The thick strike ring is acoustically crucial.
- Rossing/Perrin 1987 comparison of church/carillon/handbells and node taxonomy (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0003682X8790082X).
- Heritage Science 2021 scanned historic bell, physical model, FE, synthesis (https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-021-00620-2).
- French CEA Cast3M documented bell FEA sample with ~30 modes up to 4kHz, mode shapes and striking response: https://www-cast3m.cea.fr/html/doc_theo/mecanique_dynamique/exemples/vibrations_cloche.html (reference images/description inspected, page reader 503; not reproduced as computational proof).
- Grassmayr technical bell rib, clapper and wall thickness (https://grassmayr.at/en/produkte/glocken/geheimnis-der-glocke.php).
- A 2026 openly documented *FEM simulation* by Martin D. Adamiker with deal.II/Gmsh/SLEPc, 27,981 nodes and 104,138 quadratic tetrahedra, modal peaks 133.75/226.12/284.72 Hz: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Study5_sound.ogg . License CC BY-SA 4.0: use as research precedent, not as embedded proprietary product asset.
- Web visualization: actual Three.js glTF mesh/morph or vertex shader, local clip planes, physically correct opaque metal with explicit inspection transparency; native Web Audio PannerNode/ConvolverNode via existing owner.
