## The actual difference
R2 chiefly addresses the listener or the cards, while R3 proposes a performer who **finds, overhears, sees, misremembers, reads, receives, or stages** the exact existing Triplet. Strong candidates set a specific situation without a fresh punchline. Weak candidates claim a verifiable outside record or require a different syntax to carry the unchanged Triplet.

## Editorial warnings / weakness ranking
- **TUNE:** `An old friend said:` or `A voice on the radio:` often turns bare title-plus-POWER into falsely literal speech testimony. Test several concrete cards before approval.
- **TUNE:** `A heckler shouted:` has comic timing but generic quotation after a whole title-based mechanic can sound artificial. Restrict to event or persona with genuine voice.
- **HOLD:** Any `A politician once claimed:` / `An official statement read:` / `I watched a documentary on` can imply historical evidence for satire-card allegations. These high-risk seeds are audited, **not** on the recommended shortlist.
- **FAIL as automatic rules:** `The book was called` / `The exhibit was labelled` joins to a `Title, POWER: fragment` phrase without an explicit quotation/colon; the title alone may sound fine but the complete Triplet does not. Hold pending card-specific rewriting authorization (not granted).
- **Strong:** `I read a book about`, `I saw a film about`, `I saw a painting of`, `A friend told me about`, `I heard a rumor about`, `My diary says:`, `On the back of a napkin:` produce different speaker viewpoints with no obligatory joke. They still require a truthful-to-card class match.
- **Voice guidance:** A dry narrator can underplay the discovered line; a forgetful voice can use uncertainty, a listener can fill ellipses. Fluff-o-lect is opt-in and does not change a single canonical Triplet or pretend game mechanics.
