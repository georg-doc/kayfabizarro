Track Architecture Concept

Georg's verdict on the v0.9.1 pit: “there is no design or architecture concept at all, not even the transitions are conceived.” He is right. Every transition so far was a parameter blend: widths and offsets interpolated, barriers sunk and raised. This page proposes the missing construction system and draws the transition modules to scale, before any more geometry is built. Georg approves or changes it; Claude Design then designs form and look on top of it.

1 · construction system

Four layers, built in this order

L0 Deck
One structural slab per connected area. Where lanes run side by side, split or merge, they share one deck. The deck outline is smooth (no steps) and its free ends are rounded. It carries everything above and is the only thing supports and terrain attach to.
L1 Surface
Paint and materials on the deck: driving lanes, kerbs, hatched gore islands, entry, exit and blend lines, speed-limit lines, box markings, runoff (gravel, grass). Lanes are defined by paint, not by separate ribbons.
L2 Edge furniture
Barrier runs, pit walls and fences stand on the deck as separate objects. Every run has two designed ends: a crash cushion, a rounded cap, or a flare into runoff. Nothing sinks into the road and reappears.
L3 Scenery
Garages, pit building, gantries, stands, billboards. They sit on anchors that the core emits (the pit boxes already exist) and never touch the driving surface.
R1 · one deck

Lanes closer than 9 m edge to edge share one deck; the gap between them is a hatched gore on that deck. There are no open gaps between lanes.

R2 · the main line is sacred

At a pit lane, Weiche or exit, the through lane keeps its width and line. The other lane leaves it, never the other way round.

R3 · designed ends

Every barrier and wall run starts and ends with a terminal: a cushion at gore noses, a rounded cap at ramp lips. No stubs, no sunk barriers.

R4 · paint tells the story

Transitions are readable from paint first: gore hatch, entry and exit lines, blend line, limiter line, chevrons. Geometry follows the paint, not the other way round.

R5 · parallel families

Road edge, kerb, barrier and deck edge move together as one offset family. Bands never pinch or fold against each other.

R6 · modules on straights

Junction modules (pit, Weiche) start and end on straight road. Curves carry width changes only. This keeps every junction a clean, testable piece.

6 · modules without drawings yet

Loop and tunnel portal: rules first

Loop (classic, rollercoaster logic)

  • Stands free on its own plinth (Sockel). No suspension or beams in the track (Georg 27.09).
  • Lead-in straight of at least the loop height, with the magnet run arrow. Height 100 m by default (v0.9).
  • In the loop the barriers become slim rails. Deck and rails form one family (R5) with a designed start and end (R3).

Tunnel portal

  • A headwall or collar in the tube's own ring shape (existing rule).
  • Outside, the barrier ends at the collar with a cap (R3). Inside, the edge becomes a service strip and wall.
  • Deck and road run through unchanged (R2).
6b · design language (Georg's review of the S4 Phase A look, 27.09)

One mental concept, big simple forms

Georg rejected the first Claude Design pass: the barrier design had no concept and mixed colours and patterns; the clay marks looked like bugs when enlarged; the pale top on the brown barrier was wrong; everything felt fragmented and not cartoony; the ramp's belly bulge was bad; there were no cartoon pillars. These become rules for every module on this page:

D1 · one edge idea

The whole edge is one thing, understood at a glance (for example "Ein Guss": road and edge moulded as one body). Variants change proportion, never the idea.

D2 · two colours per world

Road and body. Paint (lines, gore hatch, kerbs in tight curves) is the only extra colour. No pale top edges, no extra thin bands.

D3 · clay at toy scale

Marks and dents stay small relative to the piece, on walls and edges only, never on the driving surface. Nothing may read as a rendering bug at 25 m/s or in a close-up.

D4 · big, few, chunky

Cartoony means few large rounded forms and bold proportions, not many small parts. Signs, cushions and pillars are chunky enough to read from the driver's seat.

D5 · taut, not bulging

Rounded ends and ramps are taut curves (bullnose, catch lip). No belly bulges and no pointed "canoe" tips.

D6 · supports belong to the look

Every raised deck stands on cartoon pillars from the same family (plinth, body, collar; Georg's classic cylinder by default). Loops stand free on a plinth.
