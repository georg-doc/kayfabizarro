## 1. North Star — Centuries on the clock, nobody will quit
These Adventurers are an ancient, recognizable fantasy-RPG party in a **mutually dependent, century-old work marriage**. They have been raiding dungeons for so long that their hero's journey has become an office routine: quest briefs, loot accounting, lunch breaks, shift changes, weekend raids, complaint forms, wiping and replaying. They alternately resent, defend, sabotage and save one another. Every member believes they are the only professional.

**They know their class extremely well; they are terrible at coordinating.** Their failures come from overperforming correct *individual* RPG behaviors at the wrong moment, not from random stupidity. Sometimes a hard-earned cooperative success should be possible; otherwise the group loses dramatic tension. Their centuries of service is a mythic comic conceit, not a demand for a simulated multi-century calendar.

The Guild feels like a genuinely lived tavern/workplace. Outside raid hours, members shop, boast, heal, argue about responsibility, eat, repair gear and take side jobs across other KFB islands; players can meet them alone asking where the missing party went. Characters have lives, reputations and relationships **before the player arrives**.

## 3. Relationship engine as comedy, not a dependency graph
The group is held together by **shared history + asymmetric dependency + mutual blackmail + occasional genuine loyalty**, not generic hostility. Design possible pair tensions:
- Knight ↔ Mage: "Go now" versus "the opening rotation requires preparation"; both are right about separate hazards and wrong about timing.
- Knight ↔ Cleric: charges ahead, out of range for healing; later publicly thanks everyone except the rescuer.
- Ranger ↔ Rogue: track-reader follows nature, treasure-reader follows profit; the only real map ends up with the missing Ranger.
- Mage ↔ Barbarian: elaborate systems versus immediate test; Barbarian accidentally validates a theory nobody wants validated.
- Cleric ↔ entire group: endlessly indispensable, blamed for delayed healing, praised only during raids; secret loyalty and exhaustion.
- Rogue / Hooded question: identity-switch satire ("it's not theft, it's reconnaissance") only if the Atlas source confirms a coherent role swap.
Keep **affection beneath the contempt**: one member guards a sleeping teammate, knows a friend's real fear, or silently leaves the rarest loot for them.

## 4. Recurring scene grammar / failure spirals (story prompts, not dialogue pool entries)
Each beat can begin as banal working life and escalate into mythic RPG kayfabe. Illustrative physically choreographable moments:
1. **The Briefing Pull**: Knight sprints past a "do not engage" marker mid-meeting; camera stays on the other five frozen in silence. A distant battle horn and one skeptical brow convey the joke without a line.
2. **The Extended Cast**: Cleric begins a great ritual in the tavern; everyone leaves to raid; on their return the incense is still burning and Cleric is still reciting the same elaborate spell.
3. **The Butterfly Detour**: Ranger drifts after a harmless glowing bird, returns with an astonishing rare artifact but no party. On other islands the Ranger asks strangers for directions to a Dungeon that nobody has heard of.
4. **Need / Greed / Love**: nobody fights the boss, but everybody rehearses their rightful claim to its future loot; Rogue has already auctioned the chest, the Finance banker insured the auction and the dragon claims ownership.
5. **Mythic Key as Attendance Card**: the mystical dungeon key looks like a punch clock. Its most fearsome quality is its weekly affix and bureaucratic sign-in.
6. **The "One More Run" Century**: after 200 years of failure, a party member announces it is their last raid. Everyone knows they will meet at the tavern for the next shift. The original promise may predate their oldest surviving character sheets.
7. **The Dungeon Master Breaks the Fourth Wall**: a dungeon builder physically slides a stone module into place while Mage complains that today's traps were patched during combat. A real Dungeon Generator source only supplies geometry once its actual owner authorizes.
8. **Raid Wipe at the Open Vault**: all die defending a chest while a gatekeeper quietly moves the label to another vault. Resurrection or respawn occurs through existing world/graveyard rules when available, not a newly invented death system.
9. **The Off-Shift Hero**: Knight, usually reckless, holds a door while the others escape; the audience realizes the class caricature has a sincere core. This makes the next disastrous boast worth watching.
10. **Wrong Island After a Portal**: a side door enters the Finance vault, Graveyard or UFO instead of the proper dungeon; the party still applies dungeon tactics with dangerous confidence. Cross-world travel uses existing Portal/Instance owners, not a new route.

These are **optional social/physical scene seeds**, not a required per-episode checklist, fixed class outcomes or canned running catchphrases. Humor follows credible stakes, gestures, silence and relationship fallout (source-based KFB Cartoon Acting North Star).

## 6. One actor / many stages / many masks — cross-island role continuity
**Key user author direction:** the meta-person remains the *same KFB Resident* even when its current Kayfabe role, quest costume, prop, faction pose or social setting changes. Think theatrical repertory, not one duplicated NPC per biome.
- Same canonical actor identity and history; distinct context-specific *portrayals*: Guild Mage in tavern, moonlighting consultant at the Finance vault, bewildered "science officer" at Hunky/Dory's UFO, philosophical narrator in the Plato cave. Role does not necessarily equal profession.
- **Minimal remixes**: actual sourced hat, robe, staff, badge, ledger, blaster, holographic prop, lighting, pose, voice/timing preference. Avoid head swapping or rig graft unless its isolated donor benefit and compatibility justify Frankensteining.
- Context can change how a Resident *plays its claim*: costume/acting register and deck-context shift; on return the person remembers plausible events and social consequences.
- Witch, Cleric and Lorekeeper can be *guest roles*, not silently recast duplicates. Resident Atlas and the existing Character/Motion/EyeRig/Face owners remain truth.
