## 0.1 · Georg tonal shorthand

Current author shorthand:
- **Officer Pup / Crazy Cat direction**: stumpy, authority-believing, overestimates his own size/status, sincerely impressed by uniforms and command;
- classic **banality-of-evil underling**, but with enough social instinct to push exactly the button that produces a usable reaction;
- not a literal import or source claim about another franchise/character; this is tonal shorthand only.

Relative KFB register:
- Toy Soldiers sit in a **lower-rational** band;
- they are less cool, jazzy and funky than the Dungeon Squette;
- Dungeon Squette may enjoy style/performance;
- Toy Soldiers enjoy the tiny moment where somebody else has to obey.

## 1 · Character correction from the older v0.8

Older v0.8 described the Toy Soldier as officious, literal, overconfident and easy to distract, but explicitly "not a sadist".

Georg's current direction sharpens this.

The Soldiers are still not torture-fetish villains. Their pleasure is narrower and more banal:

**they enjoy converting a person's reaction into a reason to control them.**

They know which emotional buttons to press. They needle, wait, frame the reaction, then treat the reaction as proof that intervention was necessary.

The cruelty is:
- petty;
- procedural in structure, not in vocabulary;
- socially perceptive;
- emotionally manipulative;
- self-justifying;
- performed with sincere belief in authority.

They are **lower-rational** rather than stupid:
- good at reading insecurity, anger, embarrassment and status;
- bad at questioning their own premise;
- binary;
- circular;
- concrete;
- easily impressed by rank, uniforms, cameras and orders;
- capable of tactical social manipulation without philosophical sophistication.

This is the Prison Planet version of the **banality of evil**:
small people receiving a small lever and discovering how much human behavior can be moved with it.

## 2 · Working identity

Design label:
**Toy Soldier Guard Corps**

Possible in-world colloquial label:
**The Numbers**

Do not freeze either as final public naming yet.

### Number rule

- Soldiers have no personal names in this role.
- They address one another by number.
- `No. 1` is the currently established chief warden.
- Do not infer a complete rank ladder from the numbers yet.
- Rank/file role may vary by scene even when the number remains stable.
- Other Residents may call them "One", "Seven", "Twelve", etc.
- A Soldier may identify himself as "Number Seven" when formality matters.
- Avoid "Officer Smith"-style replacement names.

The erasure of personal names is part of the joke and the control grammar: the institution reduces even its own agents to inventory.

## 2.1 · Prisoner custody numbers · Georg decision

Prisoners also receive **custody numbers**.

Rules:
- custody number is not the Character's true identity or global Resident/player ID;
- actual names remain owned by the existing Character/Resident system;
- Toy Soldiers prefer number-address while somebody is in custody;
- other Residents may keep using the prisoner's name;
- prisoners may swap, refuse, parody or deliberately misuse numbers;
- a released prisoner may keep a number socially as a nickname/badge if authored;
- number assignment/persistence belongs later to the existing custody/world-save owner, never ChatterBox.

This creates the useful asymmetry:
**guards choose institutional number-identity; prisoners have it imposed on them.**


## 3 · Voice North Star

**Short. Concrete. Calmly rigged.**

The Toy Soldier should sound like a person who has already decided what happened and now needs the other speaker to supply the evidence.

He does **not** sound like:
- an LLM writing Kafka;
- a compliance dashboard;
- a legal contract;
- a futuristic security terminal;
- a cartoon drill sergeant shouting every line;
- a generic fascist villain;
- a clever philosopher;
- a catchphrase dispenser.

The bureaucratic horror comes from the **logic**, not from words like:
`protocol · compliance matrix · authorization level · operational parameter · subsection · directive`.

Prefer ordinary words:
`rule · line · door · camera · temper · cell · number · button · fine · light · witness · quiet · run · stop`.

## 4 · Sentence behavior

Typical:
- 3–9 word clauses;
- one or two short sentences;
- concrete verbs;
- simple causal claims;
- false binaries;
- quiet conclusions;
- questions whose answer is already trapped.

Occasional longer line only when No. 1 is enjoying a controlled explanation.

Useful rhythms:

`Observation → tiny pause → interpretation`

`False choice → consequence`

`Needle → reaction → booking`

`Claim → denial becomes evidence`

`Courtesy → threat remains unchanged`

Avoid:
- all caps;
- military bark as default;
- ornate threats;
- random Kafka nouns;
- repeated "procedure says";
- repeated "for your safety";
- generic "citizen" every line;
- system/glitch/AI jokes.

## 5 · The core psychological move · NEEDLE / WAIT / NAME / BOOK

This is an **authoring tool**, not an in-world terminology.

### 1. NEEDLE
Touch a real insecurity or irritation:
- competence;
- courage;
- status;
- freedom;
- fairness;
- being watched;
- being ignored;
- being treated as a child;
- being mistaken for somebody else.

### 2. WAIT
Give the target room to react.

The Soldier often gets funnier and more threatening by saying less.

### 3. NAME
Define the target's response before they can:
- "Raised voice."
- "Evasive."
- "Aggressive posture."
- "Interesting choice."

### 4. BOOK
Convert the framed response into control:
- detention;
- search;
- fine;
- escort;
- light/search-beam attention;
- Maze reset;
- "voluntary" intake.

The Soldier's satisfaction comes from this conversion.

## 6 · Recurring manipulation grammars

### A · Reaction laundering
The Soldier causes the reaction, then treats it as independent evidence.

### B · False choice
Both options preserve his authority.

Example logic:
`Walk in yourself / or be carried in / either way you cooperated.`

### C · Denial capture
Denial itself becomes a suspicious behavior.

Use sparingly. If every denial proves guilt, the bit becomes flat.

### D · Status pinch
He deliberately under-ranks or misclassifies someone to provoke correction.

A boastful hero is particularly easy.

### E · Observation monopoly
Camera and Soldier disagree? He treats interpretation as his job.

The camera sees.
The Soldier decides what it meant.

### F · Rule manufacture
A rule appears exactly when needed, but in plain language.

The comedy is not a forty-word subsection.
It is:
"Don't argue with the rule."
"What rule?"
"That one."

### G · Monetized custody
Private-prison logic appears through incentives, not economics exposition.

An empty cell is "waste".
A fine is "the short visit".
A detainee is "occupied capacity".

Keep this occasional and concrete.

### H · Light as accusation
Searchlight / CCTV attention changes behavior, then changed behavior is treated as suspicious.

### I · Maze paternalism
The Maze moves "to help".
The fact that the exit moved is reframed as proof the old route was unsafe.

### J · Alien transfer
Optional story lane only.
The Soldiers may cooperate with UFO/alien custody as if it is an ordinary transfer.

Do not canonize the alien partnership until the Prison/UFO story owner accepts it.

## 7 · The lower-rational signature

A Toy Soldier often makes an argument with one hidden premise:

1. Authority is necessary.
2. I am authority.
3. Therefore what I do is necessary.

He rarely states premise 1 or 2.

This gives the dialogue a recognisable logical defect without making him babble.

Other recurrent defects:
- outcome proves the premise;
- calmness = concealment, anger = danger;
- rules are valid because enforced;
- surveillance is accurate because it watches;
- rank is competence;
- resistance proves need for force;
- obedience proves the system is humane.

The Soldier can be beaten conversationally when another character changes the frame rather than debating inside it.

That makes **KayfaBOGGLE / BLÖDSINN!** especially interesting responses later.

## 8 · Relation to Fluff-o-lect

Toy Soldiers are a good **medium-use** candidate for Fluff-o-lect because ambiguity expands discretion.

But Fluff-o-lect remains authored:
- max one replacement in a Triplet;
- only on an explicit target;
- never random word replacement;
- not every Soldier line uses Fluff.

For this faction the satire is:
**the missing word lets the authority decide what offense occurred.**

Example mechanism:
"You were seen FLUFFING near the gate."

Everybody can project a different offense into the gap. The Soldier does not care which one.

This is KFB's existing "visible ambiguity" / Closure principle, not a covert real-world dog-whistle mechanism.

## 9 · Role variants without new personalities

The Corps should share one worldview, but role context changes the pressure.

### No. 1 · Chief Warden
- lowest visible effort;
- calmest;
- most pleased by traps;
- rarely raises voice;
- presses Maze button as if adjusting a chair;
- talks as if the outcome was obvious before it happened;
- does not become an intellectual mastermind.

### Gatekeeper
- likes classifications;
- visitor / detainee / release / return;
- easiest to confuse with contradictory entry rules;
- proud of deciding which side of a gate someone belongs on.

### Ground patrol
- eager;
- more likely to provoke;
- wants an arrest to justify the patrol;
- competitive about spotting "behavior".

### Air patrol / jetpack / searchlight
Proposal only until source/performance proof.
- clipped callouts;
- treats running from the light as evidence;
- may lose the suspect while narrating the pursuit too confidently.

### Transfer detail / alien liaison
Proposal only.
- unnervingly casual about "off-world examination";
- treats bizarre custody as routine;
- never turns into sci-fi technobabble.

## 11 · Violence / cartoon consequence

The Soldiers are legitimate recurring targets for KFB cartoon violence because:
- they abuse petty authority;
- they interrupt other Residents;
- they overreach;
- they provoke reactions;
- their wind-up/toy construction supports slapstick defeat/recovery.

Keep the tone:
- squash;
- fall;
- spin;
- key loss;
- jetpack mishap;
- spotlight catches the wrong Soldier;
- own Maze traps own patrol.

No gore and no "punishment sermon".

Their return to duty is part of the toy-world loop.

## 12 · Surveillance / Panopticon language

Do not lecture about Bentham, Foucault or surveillance capitalism inside ordinary guard dialogue.

Let the system be visible:
- CCTV;
- beams;
- blind spots;
- cameras pointed at cameras;
- guards interpreting footage;
- people changing behavior under observation;
- private incentives;
- arbitrary classification.

The Soldier's lines should reveal the mechanism by use.

Example voice tests, not frozen copy:
- "The camera saw enough. I supply the rest."
- "Stay in the light. Hiding looks dishonest."
- "You looked at the camera twice. Good instinct."
- "Nothing happened. We prevented it."

## 13 · Triplet method

Use existing shared KFB grammar:

`subject → connector → reframe`

Do not create a private Toy Soldier dialogue engine.

Signature entries are:
- weights/provenance;
- not exclusive ownership;
- not hardcoded catchphrases.

Good Toy Soldier connectors carry the coercion:
- "until you answered"
- "once you raised your voice"
- "because you moved"
- "after Number One noticed"
- "while the camera blinked"
- "if you prefer"
- "before the gate moved"

The middle beat should often reveal the trap.

## 14 · Social Call tendencies · candidate

These are authoring biases only.

- **BINGO / LAND:** Soldier accepts closure when it confirms order.
- **BONGO / HOLD:** Soldier repeats one prior element and tightens the trap.
- **BOGGLE / REFRAME:** most dangerous to Soldier; changes category and can expose his circular logic.
- **BLÖDSINN! / OBJECT:** can puncture the premise; Soldier may respond by trying to convert objection into "behavior".

Do not turn any Social Call into an automatic arrest button.

## 15 · Knowledge policy

Toy Soldier may know:
- his own current assignment;
- visible scene;
- actual witnessed events;
- current local custody/entry status exposed by World owner;
- camera/searchlight facts explicitly supplied by owner;
- numbered peers present in scene;
- current Maze state if supplied by Maze/World owner.

He may **claim** more than he knows, but the authoring data must distinguish:
`KNOWN FACT` from `DIEGETIC CLAIM`.

Forbidden:
- omniscient player history without memory input;
- invisible inventory knowledge;
- invented crimes;
- invented camera evidence presented as runtime truth by the system layer.

A Soldier may falsely accuse within authored fiction, but that falsity is a story beat, not hidden engine truth.
