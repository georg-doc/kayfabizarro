## Newly inspected original government documents
**R2D-01 | CIA Office of Medical Services · December 2004 guidelines (C06541536).**
- Original 34-page scan: https://www.thetorturedatabase.org/files/foia_subsite/pdfs/cia_production_c06541536_oms_guidelines_on_medical_and_psychological_support_to_detainee_rendition_interrogation_and_detention.pdf
- Release imprint **2016/06/10**, document dated **December 2004**, ACLU-RDI 6498, original CIA guidelines for rendition/interrogation/detention medical staff. **Original screenshot pages PDF 0 and 8 visually seen**, other pages searched as text but screenshot fetches failed and are not counted.
- PDF index 0 printed page 1: OMS officer accompanies each rendition detail; initial clinical exam, injury documentation, sedation monitoring, emergency assistance; document declares a high standard of care and claims authority to change handling to avert severe/permanent injury.
- PDF index 8 printed page 9: OMS medical/psychological signoff for interrogations, requirement for physician present during waterboarding, physician/PA roles; references then-DOJ legal interpretation of lasting mental harm. This is a CIA **normative guideline**, not independent evidence that every detainee received adequate care or informed consent.
- PDF index 14–15 (OCR/text only): sleep deprivation guidelines reference detainee observations, temporary cognitive disturbances, and extrapolate from sleep studies; repeatedly notes lack of published research for the specific reconditioning circumstances and need for medical judgment.
- PDF index 16–19 (OCR/text only): waterboard practical limits; explicitly acknowledges very few individual observed cases and heterogeneity, with **limited hard data** to quantify risk or benefit. Crucially PDF index **19**, original printed p.20, explicitly says observations of each application should be documented in detail **to inform future medical judgments and recommendations**.
- This is **direct documentary evidence of clinical monitoring and operational feedback/intended learning**, but not by itself confirmation of formally designed human research or a completed control group experiment.
- Distinguish the internal clinical claims that practices were 'medically acceptable' from externally validated outcomes; several practices described would fall under severe ethical scrutiny independently of claimed safeguards.

**R2D-02 | Chief of CIA Office of Medical Services · retrospective account (C06541727).**
- Original declassified **89-page** primary institutional memoir/review: https://assets.aclu.org/live/uploads/publications/oms_summary.pdf
- Release stamp **2018/08/13–14**, title *Summary and Reflections of Chief of Medical Services on OMS Participation in the RDI Program*, manuscript original date not securely confirmed by this reading.
- PDF **index 52** OCR/text inspected; visual screenshot pages 50–51 failed (image count **0**). The narrative identifies December 2003 policy changes lowering authorized sleep deprivation periods after one reported hallucinatory episode, and February 2004 updated OMS rules. This is a **participant's retrospective institutional account**, not independent prospective surveillance data. The document labels proposed rationales and also frames public accounts as distorted; treat those assertions as the author's position, not settled truth.
- The retrospective supplies a **new source-to-source comparison target**: version evolution (2003 draft; Feb 2004 expansion; Dec 2004 released guidelines) vs actual specific incidents and official authorization. It cannot replace the dated standalone guidelines.

**R2D-03 | CIA Inspector General special review, May 7 2004 (C05856717).**
- NSA hosted **162-page declassified original**: https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/4454525/Document-07-CIA-IG-Special-Review-on.pdf ; archive catalog https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/16551-document-07-cia-ig-special-review
- Rendered page originals **PDF index 1 (routing sheet), 15 (printed section p.9), 30 (printed p.24)** inspected as page images, in addition to catalogue. Full 162 pages NOT analyzed.
- At PDF index **15**, the IG recounts an **early-1980s CIA Human Resource Exploitation training program**, a later documented interrogation doctrine link worth independent archive comparison. **This alone does not prove direct program continuity from 1950s MKUltra or the alleged Monarch program.**
- At index **30**, section about senior CIA briefings in 2003 describes the Agency General Counsel's recorded representation that a 2003 expanded use of EIT was approved by senior officials. This is an **internal description/claim about authorization**, not independent validation of legality or that briefed principals had no reservations.
- The IG's recommendation is also to seek revised legal authorization reflecting actual practices, according to the NSA archival catalog. This records internal recognition of a mismatch between approved and performed acts.

## Medical evidence triangle (time/owner/certainty)
1. **2004 OMS guideline** defines what CIA personnel were meant to monitor and record; direct original, explicitly describes need to inform later evaluations.
2. **2005 DOJ OLC Bradbury memo** (already S47, official 20p PDF) relies upon CIA-provided medical/psychological observations and claims experience with >25 detainees to assess legality of combined interrogations; **OLC legal conclusion depends on assumptions and CIA inputs**, not clinical evidence audit.
3. **2010 PHR analysis** (already S46) interprets these feedback and comparative statements as potentially meeting criteria of unconsented human-subject research. PHR acknowledged that original subject-level data and a formal trial/IRB record were not publicly available.
4. **CIA OMS retrospective** adds first-person policy chronology and acknowledgment of revisions due to observed adverse effects; it cannot automatically validate ethical or medical efficacy claims.
5. **CIA 2004 IG** supplies governance/accountability context and an earlier documented doctrinal cousin (Human Resource Exploitation program), not proof of covert 1950s-to-2000s institutional succession.

Thus **documented**: guidelines, monitoring, intended future learning, claimed comparative use in OLC and reported reactions/revisions. **Unresolved**: existence of a formal prospective experiment design, original collected individual data, patient consent, research ethics approvals, and the causal inference from those medical observations to technique efficacy.

## Atlas pilot: chronological/documented versus associative links
1. `CIA OMS Dec 2004 guidelines` → `DOJ OLC May 2005`: **INFORMS_LEGAL_ANALYSIS**, supported by original source documents; the medical claims are attributed CIA inputs.
2. `DOJ OLC May 2005` → `PHR 2010`: **INTERPRETED_AS_POSSIBLE_HUMAN_RESEARCH**, a specific later ethical/legal thesis, not settled experimental proof.
3. `CIA OMS Dec 2004` ↔ `CIA OMS retrospective`: **HISTORICAL_VERSION_COMPARISON**, reported guideline revisions, independent underlying original incident data still needed.
4. `CIA Human Resource Exploitation 1980s` ↔ `MKUltra 1950s`: **WARBURG_NEIGHBOR/OPEN_HYPOTHESIS** for methods/organizations; direct personnel/program descendant not shown.
5. `Guantánamo BSCT 2002` ↔ `CIA OMS program`: **MECHANISM_SIMILARITY** for clinical-advisory participation; different agencies/command and dates, no direct shared owner demonstrated.
6. `one 2004 OMS memorandum` in `medical ethics / legal policy / institutional history / public media`: **APPEARS_IN_FIELD**, Gabriel-inspired interpretive pluralism, fields have different evidence/standards. Not an equivalence of all interpretations.
