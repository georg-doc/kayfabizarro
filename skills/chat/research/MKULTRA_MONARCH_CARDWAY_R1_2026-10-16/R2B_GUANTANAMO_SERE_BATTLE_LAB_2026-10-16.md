## High-value finding: 'Battle Lab' is literally in the Senate report
**Original:** U.S. Senate Armed Services Committee, *Inquiry into the Treatment of Detainees in U.S. Custody*, S. Prt. 110-54, report adopted 20 Nov 2008, published 2009. Official contents at https://bookstore.gpo.gov/products/inquiry-treatment-detainees-us-custody-report-november-20-2008 and report PDF https://www.armed-services.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/Detainee-Report-Final_April-22-2009.pdf .
Section **III**, printed p.38 is titled **“Guantanamo Bay as a ‘Battle Lab’ for New Interrogation Techniques”**; subchapters address BSCT formation (p.38), psychological/psychiatric members, USASOC/JPRA-SERE contact, September 2002 training, a BSCT interrogation policy memorandum and Mohammed al-Qahtani (through pp.57ff). The title exists in the official contents (verified); it is **not independently a finding of lawful/IRB-regulated medical research**. Full approx. 200+ page report PDF was **not ingested** owing to web size limit (ca 16MB). Reuse the separate accessible 19-page **official SASC executive summary** for inspected details:
https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/torturingdemocracy/documents/20081211.pdf .
Original scan/image **pages index 2 (printed xiii), 3 (xiv), 10 (xxi) and 17 (xxviii)** visually inspected (four rendered pages). Index 2 establishes JPRA/SERE origin, index 3 explicitly records DoD motive to 'reverse engineer' resistance methods and SERE psychologist involvement; 10 reports Navy counsel objections and Guantánamo consequences; 17 shows Senate Conclusion 13 (Rumsfeld approval a direct cause of abuse) and transfer to other war zones. This is a congressional **institutional finding**, not evidence of a specific named 1950s personnel line to MKUltra.

## Leaks, investigators, dissent
### 4. 'Detainee 063', Mohammed al-Qahtani
TIME, June 12/20 2005, reports receipt of an approximately **84-page SECRET/ORCON contemporaneous interrogation log** spanning late 2002–Jan 2003. Original *full log* was **not located/read in this checkpoint**; TIME's newsroom report describes minute-by-minute documentation, deprivation, intimidation, demeaning measures and repeated medical measurements: https://content.time.com/time/press_releases/article/0%2C8599%2C1071230%2C00.html and https://time.com/3624326/inside-the-interrogation-of-detainee-063/ . **Source tier currently: journalist description of a leaked primary source, NOT original file directly inspected.** Prioritize finding an accessible direct scan of the log; do not claim 84/83 page variants resolved.
**Medical consequences and high-level assessment:** military commission convening authority **Susan J. Crawford** said in a 14 Jan 2009 interview that al-Qahtani's cumulative treatment legally constituted torture; she had personally reviewed military records and did not refer his case for prosecution. Reported hospital episodes and marked bradycardia are attributed to records/Crawford, not an independently inspected chart in this checkpoint: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/national/2009/01/14/detainee-tortured-says-us-official/da3f2eb2-f373-40f7-ad7a-5711e04bf31d/ . Pentagon spokesman at the time said older reviews had found techniques lawful and later policies were tightened. **Crawford's interview ≠ a clinical study**.

### 5. Inspector General, DOJ/FBI, May 2008
Original official 438-page full report https://oig.justice.gov/sites/default/files/archive/special/s0805/final.pdf , detected but full scan **not ingested or image-inspected** due length/cache. Accessible **own-agency summary** https://oig.justice.gov/archives/semiannual/0811/fbi.htm describes survey of **more than 1,000 FBI employees** serving at Guantánamo, Afghanistan and Iraq, with **more than 300 reporting direct or secondhand information** about harsh interrogation by military personnel. Do not imply 300 direct eyewitnesses **at Guantánamo**. OIG reports objections and notes it found no supporting evidence of widespread FBI complicity; most agents avoided direct participation. This is important **internal disagreement**, not all U.S. agencies acting as one unit.

### 6. Clinical advisory teams (BSCT)
Medical/journalistic scholarly discussion: *Doctors and Interrogators at Guantanamo Bay*, New England Journal of Medicine, 2005, https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMp058145 . Search-accessible passage describes a **psychologist and psychiatrist** initially staffing the BSCT, profiles/recommendations/observations of interrogations and involvement with detainee 063. NEJM full page returned access **403** in this session; scope is exact excerpt plus official Senate contents/BSCT administrative record. Original 2002 BSCT Standard Operating Procedures appear in Wikimedia Commons (18-page scanned PDF), https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BSCT_Standard_Operating_Procedures_--_2002-11-11.pdf ; **not inspected**. ### 7. Human-subject experiments hypothesis
Physicians for Human Rights (PHR), *Experiments in Torture*, June 2010, https://phr.org/our-work/resources/experiments-in-torture-3/ and linked original research PDF https://phr.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/06/Experiments_in_Torture.pdf . PHR states it found documentary indicators of possible **nonconsensual human-subject research** by CIA medical personnel during the CIA **enhanced interrogation program**, including possible use of resulting observations to shape procedures and legal representations. **This is an NGO clinical/ethical analytic allegation, not a completed criminal or regulatory finding of systematic experiments at Guantánamo**. The CIA detention programme and DoD-run GTMO facility overlap in policy history but are different detention/command chains.
Extra independent context: Constitution Project bipartisan task force 2013, >100 interviews and site visits but **no subpoena access to classified records**; https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/publications/report-constitution-project-s-task-force-detainee-treatment . This is independent secondary investigation, not a contemporaneous CIA primary record.
