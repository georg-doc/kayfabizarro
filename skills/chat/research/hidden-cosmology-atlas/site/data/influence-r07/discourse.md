## Three decisions the research supports

1. **Call the three-dice board “Discourse Focus”.** Its Resources/Rules/Influence corners privilege access to types of power; they are not ordered positions on one political question. Keep the rectangular market window as an A/B gameplay comparator, likewise labelled an abstraction. A genuine Overton test would need a single issue and ordered policy positions. Neither layout is a truth meter or validated psychological model.
2. **Keep action and justification separate.** The engine records who spent Favor, obtained a node and changed its dependencies. A parallel, optional line gives the official justification. Never award points for acting ability, ideological agreement or telling a convincing lie. The satire emerges when two incompatible justifications accompany the same observable transaction.
3. **Allow an impersonal system to reproduce the frame.** Solo should use disclosed priorities and an event schedule; it need not impersonate an omniscient puppet master. Public focus can persist because players profit from repeating it, and can change through either player action or a neutral event. No extra persuasion currency or three separate R/A/F subsystems are justified by these sources.

These are **design inferences**, not scientifically tested gameplay. Counterplay: pivot into a different channel, contest the shared focus or retain Favor for an exposed dependency. Solo uses the same cost calculation as players; its information and tie-breaks must be explicit. For 5–6 players, one bounded reaction window avoids making narrative discussion a mandatory negotiation phase.

## Comparative reading: what actually changes between lenses

**Yurchak → Curtis:** The useful shared problem is the gap between authoritative presentation and lived complexity. Their causal structures must remain distinct: ethnographic reconstruction of reproduced forms versus a film essay spanning institutions and decades. For gameplay, both support visible contradiction, but Yurchak supplies a counterweight to the assumption that one mastermind designed every effect. Curtis’s own criticism of oppositional culture also permits satire to turn on the player who claims to be outside the system.

**Overton → agenda setting → framing:** Acceptable policy, important topic and interpretation of an issue are three different objects. The RAF triangle currently most closely represents a **game abstraction of focus/access**. It should not imply that high frequency proves a proposition true or that a high Resonance die diagnoses an audience. A future policy spectrum belongs in an optional module after the simpler prototype has been played.

**Machiavelli → Sun Tzu → Hegel:** Reputation, strategically concealed intention and conceptual contradiction are useful neighbors, but not interchangeable doctrines. A fictional crisis card can be titled “The Solution Was Ready”; its source note must say the game invented this particular sequence. Avoid “Hegel’s mind-control formula”. No extra philosophy phase is necessary.

## Fiction bridges that keep the evidence layer intact

These are wording directions, not additional cards beyond the56-slot budget:

- **The Independent Council:** public line “Our funding has no bearing on our conclusions.” Visible transaction: sponsor changes, dependency remains. Historical neighborhood: DISC15–16; no real institution portrayed as secretly controlled without specific evidence.
- **The Solution Was Ready:** a pre-announced remedy profits from an event. A second reading is mundane preparedness or opportunism. The player action establishes what occurred in this match; the real-world source tag never upgrades a planning document to an executed attack.
- **Everyone Signed:** the same declaration survives a change of owner. The player may read the line or skip performance; the log records the control change. Neighborhood: DISC01, not an experiment claiming to reproduce Soviet society.
