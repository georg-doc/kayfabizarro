<h2>Do two essays independently establish the purpose of mBridge?</h2>
<p><strong>Finding.</strong> Two essays published sixteen months apart lead readers to exactly the same BIS project page. They document a recurring argument by the author and one institutional source for the project. The source supports cooperation on cross-border payments. The essays give that cooperation a broader geopolitical meaning.</p>

<h3>What did the issuer document?</h3>
<p>The shared URL now redirects to a BIS retrospective. A dated project report existed before both essays in September 2021. The BIS later reports real-value pilot transactions in 2022, a minimum viable product in mid-2024 and its handover to partners in October 2024. The currently displayed 2024 material postdates both essays. The founding participants named by BIS are its Innovation Hub and monetary authorities from Hong Kong, mainland China, Thailand and the UAE. Europe-wide adoption or a BRICS-wide mandate does not follow from that list. <a href="https://www.bis.org/publications/inthanon-lionrock-mbridge-building-multi-cbdc-platform-international-payments">Dated 2021 report</a> · <a href="https://www.bis.org/about/bisih/topics/cbdc/mcbdc_bridge.htm">Shared BIS page, including subsequent milestones</a>.</p>
<h3>Two readings and one discriminating test</h3>
<p><strong>Strategic reading.</strong> The trial could furnish infrastructure for a wider political currency project. <strong>POSIWID:</strong> identify which participants actually settled payments and acquired decision rights. <strong>Hanlon’s alternative:</strong> overlapping pilots and shared technology can arise from several institutions independently trying to reduce settlement delays. Repeated links in commentary can also reflect ordinary citation reuse.</p>
<p><strong>What would change the assessment?</strong> A dated decision by a European or BRICS governing body adopting mBridge for the specific bloc-wide purpose would support the stronger interpretation. A decision choosing a different infrastructure would weigh against that jurisdiction-specific prediction. The next source should come from the proposed adopter; another repost of the BIS page supplies no new decision-maker.</p>
