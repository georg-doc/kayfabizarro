## 3. Interpretation

The card compresses different surveillance mechanisms into an all-seeing backdoor. That is a dramatic premise; the inspected report supports a narrower collection process. The NB01 infographic is useful as a question map, not proof of blanket access. Neither this report nor a product label establishes that every encrypted message is readable.

Avoid both total-immunity promises and the claim that nothing helps. Protection depends on the data, adversary and access mechanism.

## 5. Solution Watch / practical action

Spend ten minutes making a one-data-item security plan before buying a new phone.

- Choose one item: private messages, location history or documents.
- List where it is stored, who can access it and the specific access you want to reduce.
- Choose one proportionate change, then set a review date.

**Keep the current device and review one unnecessary app permission** — No new hardware cost; bounded and reversible. Limit: Does not remove every provider-held record or address every adversary.

**Compare a different communication/service workflow against the same threat** — Tests whether a change actually addresses the chosen exposure. Limit: Compatibility, contacts and migration effort matter; encryption and endpoints need separate review.

**Consider alternative hardware/OS only after the threat comparison** — May fit particular needs. Limit: Above Phone, Volla and other products are leads, not tested recommendations in this pilot; no promise of anonymity.

No purchase verdict or individual legal/security guarantee.
