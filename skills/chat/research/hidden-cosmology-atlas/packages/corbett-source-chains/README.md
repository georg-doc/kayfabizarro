<h2>Who drafted the Balfour Declaration?</h2>
<p>Tom-Oliver Regenauer and James Corbett cite the same centenary website when discussing the draft sent by Walter Rothschild and the changes associated with Alfred Milner. Their published links establish a shared path to the documents. The record supports a sequence of proposals, revisions and a government decision. Who independently inspected which manuscript remains unknown.</p>
<h3>1 · Follow Regenauer’s two links</h3>
<p>In <em>Trotzki, Commonwealth und Wall Street</em> (7 February 2022), find the paragraph beginning “Sinnbildlich”. Regenauer attributes authorship to Rothschild and presents his agreement with Milner as preceding government involvement. His link labeled “Webseite” leads to the homepage of Balfour 100 (article-body link a18, PDF 045 p4-a4). Much later, a37 (PDF p9-a1) recommends Corbett’s documentary as background on the First World War. Its placement establishes the recommendation, while leaving the immediate origin of the earlier paragraph open. <a href="https://www.regenauer.press/trotzki-commonwealth-und-wall-street">Regenauer’s essay</a>.</p>
<h3>2 · Separate Corbett’s narration from his guest</h3>
<p>In the published transcript of <em>The WWI Conspiracy</em> (19 November 2018), the narrator attributes the original proposal to Rothschild and a revision to Milner. In the ensuing interview passage it is <strong>Richard Grove</strong> who points readers to the centenary site for the successive versions. The exact outgoing target in both authors’ HTML is <code>http://www.balfour100.com/</code>. Neither of these two links identifies an individual archival sheet. The centenary exhibition distinguishes the 18 July version forwarded by Rothschild from an earlier working draft. The relevant comparison is between documents, even when they are reached through the same website. <a href="https://corbettreport.com/wwi/">Corbett transcript, Balfour passage and GROVE block</a> · <a href="https://balfour100.org.uk/declaration/">Exhibition of drafts</a>.</p>
<h3>3 · Check the documentary sequence</h3>
<ul><li><strong>18 July 1917.</strong> Rothschild’s covering letter transmits a proposed formula to Balfour. A copy of the letter and draft was read into Parliament in 1947. It describes the formula as requested and seeks government approval. Submission by Rothschild is documented; sole authorship is a different claim. <a href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/1947-12-11/debates/87569bef-8402-49d4-92d6-398f31ed1c77/CommonsChamber">Hansard, 11 December 1947, search for “18th July, 1917”</a>.</li>
<li><strong>August to October 1917.</strong> Appendix II of the War Cabinet papers labels an August draft as submitted by the Foreign Secretary, followed by Milner’s 4 October draft and proposed amendments from several respondents. The first formula speaks of reconstituting Palestine as a national home; the October draft moves to a home in Palestine and adds safeguards. Foreign Office participation therefore predates this Milner version. <a href="https://balfourproject.org/bp/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/War-cabinet-minutes-Oct-1917.pdf">War Cabinet papers, Appendix II, Draft Declarations, items 1–3</a>.</li>
<li><strong>6 October and 2 November 1917.</strong> The British Library catalogues Hankey’s invitation to Rothschild to comment (Add MS 41178 A, f. 1), and Balfour’s final letter addressed to Rothschild (f. 3). The final letter communicates Cabinet approval and asks Rothschild to convey the declaration to the Zionist Federation. <a href="https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002093104">British Library catalogue</a> · <a href="https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-193242/">Final text in the UN collection</a>.</li></ul>
<h3>What the comparison resolves</h3>
<p><strong>Supported:</strong> Rothschild submitted an early proposal, Milner submitted a changed version, other parties were consulted, and Balfour conveyed the government’s decision. <strong>Beyond the evidence:</strong> that Rothschild alone composed the final declaration, or that government officials first received its wording only after Rothschild and Milner had settled it. To establish authorship of a specific clause, compare dated working copies, annotations and correspondence for that clause.</p>
