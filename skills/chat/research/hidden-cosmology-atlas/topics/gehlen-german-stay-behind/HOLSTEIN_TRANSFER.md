# The Holstein network: who proposed the handover?

Hans Mathiesen Lunding offered the West German side the network run in southeastern Schleswig-Holstein by former Wehrmacht officer Günter Toepke (also spelled Günther). The best located account places the approach in Hamburg on 8 March 1956 and identifies Adolf Heusinger as the person from whom Lunding sought help. A German file note dated 13 March is cited by researchers who used the intelligence archive. No signed German acceptance, personnel roll transferred or effective date of operational control was located.

## Following the references from the 2015 book

1. **Locate the edition.** In the [2015 contents](https://d-nb.info/1071212737/04), “Die Übernahme von ausländisch geführten Netzen” begins at printed p. 142 and the next section at 147. In [another edition](https://d-nb.info/1082213160/04) it begins on p. 182. The body and notes of pp. 142–146 were not directly available in the searched copies. Two later references explicitly recover pp. 143 and 144.
2. **Identify the sending side.** In his [interview with Stoll](https://www.telepolis.de/article/Partisanen-der-NATO-3376143.html), answer to “Wie kam es zur Übergabe”, Schmidt-Eenboom attributes transfers in the aggregate to French, Dutch and Danish networks. In [Friis and Schmidt-Eenboom, *En mærkelig forbindelse?*](https://tidsskrift.dk/frakrigogfred/article/download/163931/205347/364145), notes 36–37, the authors cite their German file for the Danish case and p. 143 of the 2015 book for the parallel process. In [Heckmann and Friis, H. M. Lunding, in *Spionage im Grenzland*](https://d-nb.info/1370626479/34), note 22 cites the 2015 book, p. 144, for Toepke’s leadership. These are overlapping investigations, including the same co-author or archive, not independent witnesses to a handover ceremony.
3. **Recover the shelfmark and locator.** BND archive **01292**, also cited **01292.e**, note concerning a meeting with **KAMMGARN**, Hamburg, memorandum date **13 March 1956**. The Danish article's note 36 specifies **file page 2** for the comparison between the Thor and Toepke networks; note 32 points to **file page 3**. Note 37 separately identifies *Die Partisanen der NATO*, p. 143. Heckmann/Friis, note 56, points back to the memorandum. Search on the shelfmark, Hamburg, both dates and both spellings Kammgarn/Kammkarn. Date variants in secondary citations include 1856 and 1957. The March 1956 reading follows notes 32 and 36 and the chronology. The year on the original sheet still needs inspection.
4. **Read the verb.** Lunding offered the network after the Schleswig-Holstein domestic security authority had noticed it. He asked for a German command authority and a position for Toepke. The Danish authors identify Heusinger as the contact. John Jensen's Toepke contribution in the [same volume](https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12657/103897/1062936WWXQ7755_Abraham%20et%20al_Spionage%20im%20Grenzland.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1), notes 30–31, distinguishes the offer from Toepke's subsequent Bundeswehr career. A military appointment proves where the officer went, not who received his agents. The Hamburg meeting predates the BND's formal establishment on 1 April. “BND” is retrospective shorthand here. The texts do not establish Heusinger's signature, Gehlen's authorization or a Dulles order for this net.
5. **Separate neighboring networks.** The Danish article distinguishes Mørch's **Thor**, already cooperating with the German side, from Lunding's operation. Its suggestion of a later **Janus** name is qualified. Use “Lunding–Toepke network, Holstein” as the stable label. Do not merge Thor, JANUS, SATURN or KIBITZ-15 on the basis of geography. For the last of these, the [April 1953 CIA document, EGPA-1092](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/KIBITZ%20%20%20VOL.%202_0147.pdf) describes closing down Kopp's project. This supplies a concrete counterexample to assuming every Allied net passed intact to the BND.
6. **Check what the other files concern.** Stoll's five-page [companion to chapter 8](https://www.geheimdienste.info/texte/Bonusmaterial_Kap_8.pdf), notes 1–4 and 10, gives his interview dates (2012 and 2013), file 4449_OT p. 68 for the LAFBw establishment order, and 4481_OT p. 75 for a 1968 relocation order. Those are valuable dated orders for later units. They cannot serve as signatures for the 1956 Holstein proposal. [Tamir Sinai](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0968344520914345), German case, notes 59–62, also distinguishes the general transition from the missing evidence for a British NORTHAG handover.
