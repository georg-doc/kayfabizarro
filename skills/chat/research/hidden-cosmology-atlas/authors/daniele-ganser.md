# Daniele Ganser · Gladio and European stay-behind research

**Author lens.** Ganser compares the creation, foreign support and democratic oversight of clandestine networks across countries. He examines claims of links to political violence in particular countries. Keep each country's program, date and underlying source attached to its own claims.

## Works and scope

- **Doctoral dissertation (2001).** The University of Basel catalogs *Operation Gladio in Western Europe and the United States* as a doctoral thesis. Ganser's book acknowledgements date his final doctoral examinations to September 2001. [Basel record](https://edoc.unibas.ch/entities/publication/4cc9b089-5822-4276-b449-35b2a03edfd6/full)
- **Comparative monograph (2005).** *NATO’s Secret Armies: Operation Gladio and Terrorism in Western Europe*. In the acknowledgements, pp. xiv–xv, Ganser identifies the Italian SIFAR paper of 1 June 1959 as his starting point, describes the difficulty of archival access, and explains his selection of 14 NATO countries. Chapters 1–3 trace the Italian attack, European revelations and institutional responses. Chapters 4–17 examine Britain, the US, Italy, France, Spain, Portugal, Belgium, the Netherlands, Luxembourg, Denmark, Norway, Germany, Greece and Turkey. Germany is chapter 15. Use the full work to compare hypotheses, including where source availability differs. [Publisher and contents](https://www.routledge.com/natos-secret-armies-operation-gladio-and-terrorism-in-western-europe/ganser/p/book/9780714685007) · [Book acknowledgements and text](https://www.bluemoonofshanghai.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/NATOs-Secret-Armies-Daniele-Ganser.pdf)
- **Source collection.** The Parallel History Project collection edited with Christian Nuenlist includes Italian military records and material from other countries and periods. Its document index supports comparison at document level. [Collection](https://www.php.isn.ethz.ch/lory1.ethz.ch/collections/colltopicfd4f.html?id=16165&lng=en&navinfo=15301)
- **Neutral states.** Ganser discusses Switzerland separately in his 2005 article on British intelligence and Swiss stay-behind. That distinction matters when comparing NATO membership with cooperation across its borders. [ETH publication record](https://css.ethz.ch/en/publications/journal-articles/details.html?id=%2Ft%2Fh%2Fe%2Fb%2Fthe_british_secret_service_in_neutral_sw)

## A traceable German attribution

In his 2006 article Ganser quotes an unnamed NATO source describing Gehlen as the intellectual originator of German stay-behind and alleging early knowledge by Adenauer. **Footnote 48** cites *Searchlight*, January 1991. The surviving OCR of issue 187 reproduces this characterization in the feature on pp. 3–6. This is one chain of testimony. [Ganser article, passage and note 48](https://www.siper.ch/assets/uploads/files/publications/Intelligence%20and%20National%20Security%20%282006%29%20-%20The%20CIA%20in%20Western%20Europe%20and%20the%20Abuse.pdf) · [Searchlight 187, digitized issue](https://www.scribd.com/document/378818633/Searchlight-187-January-1991)

The [German handover case](../topics/gehlen-german-stay-behind/README.md) independently identifies an operational role for Gehlen in 1951 and Smith's instruction to Mellenthin in 1952. Neither settles the anonymous source's broader priority claim. Coverage here is bibliographic for the dissertation, structural and selective for the monograph, passage-and-note specific for the article.
