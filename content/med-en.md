# Akt I · Setup

## What it is. {#what}

MedKayfab is the KayfaBizarro engine applied to pathophysiology. Each player picks a Character: a **clinician, a drug, an immune cell, a rogue signalling molecule, or a personified disease**. Together you build a shared Clinical Stage and push a medical Quest toward resolution.

The mechanism you perform on your turn is the mechanism you keep in your head at 4am. **No win condition. No score.** Just a pathophysiology you can explain the next morning without looking at the card.

The goal isn't recall. The goal is this: you can explain the mechanism to a frightened patient at 6am who studied no pharmacology, and they hear something true. That's the Feynman bar. You don't memorize cards. You perform mechanisms. The deck is just the record of which ones you've taken on so far.

If you know KayfaBizarro Freestyle already: same five steps, same Stage, same BLÖDSINN! The Story-Mode Die becomes the **Clinical Mode Die**, and every card carries a disease mechanism instead of a story beat.

### What you need

:::rules
Cards | Each player brings 15-20 MedKayfab cards. Shuffle into one shared deck. Characters are clinicians, drugs, cells, or personified diseases. Scene cards are clinical contexts. Quest cards are cases.
Two D6 | One *Clinical Mode Die*, one *Quest Die*. Both stay visible at all times.
Actor markers | Coins, bottle caps, a die laid sideways. Anything that sits on your Character card so the table sees who's active. *FrizzleBob says: alles erlaubt, aber bitte keine Zähne.*
Solo or 1-6 | Works solo for exam prep. Works best at 3-5 around a table. Works at any hour, but 2am hits different.
:::

## Setup {#setup}

Two minutes. No pre-reading. You learn the rules by playing the first round.

:::steps
1. Deal | Give 3 cards to each player. Keep them secret. Scrub in.
2. Characters | Everyone picks 1 card from their hand and reveals it face-up. Run the Intro Ritual for your Character. This is who you are for this episode: a cardiologist, an aspirin molecule, an arrhythmia, whatever the card says.
3. Quest Reveal | **The most caffeinated player** draws a Quest card and runs the Intro Ritual. This is the clinical case. Quest Die to **1**.
4. Stage | Stage empty. Three Scene slots open, ready to fill with clinical context as the case develops.
5. Go | First turn. The Quest Die is at 1. The patient just walked in.
:::

:::quote
"New to this? Skip the Clinical Mode Die for the first round. Everyone just introduces their Character. Once the table has found its voice, roll as normal."
:::

## The Intro Ritual {#ritual}

Every card that enters the game has to be introduced. Three beats. Thirty seconds. In character. This is not a summary, it's a performance. You only remember what you performed, not what you read.

:::ritual
Name It | Announce the card's name *and* the clinical concept it represents. Loud. "Fibby Fitz. Atrial fibrillation."
Claim It | One sentence: what is its *mechanism*? Not the treatment, the mechanism. "The atria fire chaotically. The AV node only lets some impulses through. The ventricles never know what's coming."
Power It | **Don't explain it. Perform it.** Show what this mechanism does to the patient right now. Voice the drug. Voice the disease. Make the people around you feel the arrhythmia.
:::

:::note
The Intro Ritual applies to **every card, every time**: Quest cards, Scene cards, new Characters. Always. Skip it once and somebody at the table makes you start over. They always do.
:::

:::note
**Kayfab-Finger Rule.** Point at each card as you mention it. Helps beginners, kids, and keeps the storytelling moves visible.
:::

# Akt I · Gameplay

## The Five-Step Turn {#turn}

Each turn runs the same five steps. The Clinical Mode you rolled shapes *how* you approach the mechanism, not whether you approach it. You always approach it.

:::step-card
title: The Five-Step Turn
meta: ~2 min per player
steps:
01 | Draw | Draw 1 card from the top of the shared deck. You now hold 4 cards temporarily.
02 | Roll | Roll the Clinical Mode Die. This is your Mode for this turn. Not optional. Commit to it.
03 | Play | Play 1 card: Scene card into a Stage slot (empty, or overwrite one at 3/3), or switch your Character. Always run the Intro Ritual. Return to 3 hand cards.
04 | Tell | In character, in your Mode. Weave the Stage into the clinical narrative. **Power woven in → card to discard. Power skipped → card stays on Stage.**
05 | Move | Move the Quest Die: **+2, +1, 0, or −1.** Your call. How far along is this case?
:::

## The Six Clinical Modes {#modes}

Roll the Clinical Mode Die at the start of your turn. The Mode is a frame for how you engage with the mechanism, not a constraint on what you say. If the Mode contradicts your read on the case, play that contradiction. The best teaching moments come from unexpected angles.

:::modes
1 | Morning Report | Walk through this turn's hand the way you'd read it off the chart at handover: your Actor, what each Scene contributes, where the Quest sits. Plain. Textbook. The baseline the other modes tear apart.
2 | Heel Turn | The case turns on you mid-turn. One of your cards lies, contradicts another, or hides a complication. Comorbidity, drug interaction, the patient on six other meds nobody listed. Play the twist, not the textbook version.
3 | Hand-Off | **Active recall.** Turn your Actor card face-down. Tell this turn's story, Actor, Scenes, Quest, mechanism, as if signing the patient out to the next shift. From memory. Anyone calls BLÖDSINN! if you drift. Nail it: Quest Die +1. Then turn the card back up and read the LORE aloud. No penalty for gaps, the gaps are what you came here to find.
4 | Plain Speak | **Feynman bar.** Same hand, same story, no jargon. No card-text recital, no Latin. Pick a stand-in: a worried family member, the night cleaner, your grandmother, a barista. The mechanism has to land for someone who studied no medicine. Less than a minute. Point at the cards as you go.
5 | The Real Question | Skip the monologue. Ask one genuine question about your Actor, a Scene, or the Quest, in character, to another player. Not a pimping question, a generative one. They answer in 1-2 sentences. Anyone calls BLÖDSINN! if the question was unanswerable. **No shaming, ever.**
6 | Attending AWOL | Total anarchy. The attending has left the floor. Play a card directly onto the Stage (Intro Ritual still applies) and move the Quest Die as you see fit. No Mode constraint this turn. The table watches, the table reacts.
:::

:::quote
*"You think six Modes are enough? You think we've covered every way to teach a mechanism? Invent your own. Try it next session. If it teaches better than these, send the rule to us, the deck gets sharper. These six aren't sacred. They're the ones we've tested so far."*
- Doc FrizzleBob
:::

:::quote
"Mode 3 is the one residents avoid. Mode 3 is the only one that actually works."
- Doc FrizzleBob
:::

:::sub-section
**The Real Question, the difference between pimping and asking.**

Pimping question, don't ask: *"What's the half-life of warfarin?"* That's a recall trap. The student either knows or freezes.

Genuine question, ask: *"If we give warfarin now, what's the first sign you'd watch for tonight?"* That opens a mechanism. The student can reason their way in even if they don't know the number.

If you ask a pimping question, anyone at the table calls BLÖDSINN! and you reframe before continuing.
:::

## The Clinical Stage {#stage}

The Stage holds **three Scene card slots**: the shared clinical context everyone is playing inside. Think of it as the patient's presentation: the ECG trace, the ward, the comorbidity list. Each slot starts empty.

:::rules
Empty slot | Fill it with a Scene card (Intro Ritual, then place). The players now treat that Scene as part of the shared clinical reality for the rest of the case.
Stage full (3/3) | Overwrite one slot with a *yes-and*. Pick a Scene to discard and replace it. The player who plays it reframes the case through the new Scene.
:::

### Character Switch

At Step 03, instead of playing a Scene card, you can play a new Character card. You switch the clinician, drug, or disease you embody. Run the Intro Ritual for the new Character. Your old Character exits or stays as an NPC. The Stage is unchanged. Draw back to 3 hand cards.

:::quote
"The best character switches happen when someone pulls the antidote out mid-crisis."
:::

# Akt II · Progression

## The Quest Die {#quest}

The Quest Die shows where the case is in its arc. It is not a timer, it is a clinical compass. *You decide when to move it.* A Quest Die at 1 is a patient in triage. At 5, someone is about to make a decision they cannot take back. At 6, the team closes the case.

:::quest-strip
1-2 | Anamnesis | Symptoms
3-4 | Workup | Escalation
5 | Crisis | Must give
6 | Finale | The team closes.
:::

:::ref-table
+2 | **Hard push.** The case is deteriorating fast. Use when a complication has just landed and there's no time to work up calmly.
+1 | **Steady escalation.** Default forward move. You found something new. Keep going.
0 | **Hold.** The attending needs more data. Sit in the uncertainty. This is where real clinical thinking happens.
−1 | **Retreat.** New information just changed the picture. The diagnosis was wrong. Step back and re-examine.
:::

:::note
Quest Die **never goes below 1**. When it hits 6, **the next player's turn is the Finale turn**.
:::

## The Grand Finale {#finale}

When the Quest Die hits **6**, the next player's turn is the Finale turn. Same five steps, but the Tell must close the case: diagnosis, mechanism, treatment decision. Not a summary. A performance.

:::rules
Memory | Quest card stays on the table as the case record. Whoever performed the mechanism carries it forward. The other players remember what they heard.
Clear | Stage cleared. All Scene cards to discard. The team has moved on, the next patient is on the way.
New Quest | The next player draws a Quest card and runs the full Quest Reveal ritual. New case.
Reset | Quest Die to 1. New patient, new presentation, same table.
:::

:::quote
"The Finale doesn't have to be right. It has to be confident enough that you can defend it for the next twelve hours."
- Doc FrizzleBob
:::

# Table Culture

## Social Calls {#socials}

Four calls any player can make at any time. They interrupt the current turn. They are non-negotiable: if anyone at the table calls one, the call stands.

:::socials
KayfaBINGO! | The mechanism just landed clearly. Pure praise. The active player draws an extra card. Use generously, since players learn from the moments anyone applauds, not just the moments anyone corrects.
KayfaBOGGLE? | One concrete question. *"What's the mechanism behind the rate control?"*, not *"I don't understand."* Active player answers in 1-2 sentences, in character, then continues.
KayfaBONGO! | A player described their Power as a statistic (*"blocks COX-1 by 80%"*). Anyone calls it. Active player restates as a clinical narrative: what happens to the patient, not to the number. No Quest Die effect.
BLÖDSINN! | A rule was broken: out of character, mechanism skipped, Mode ignored, or a flat-out incorrect mechanism stated with confidence. Majority decides. If it stands: **Quest Die does not move**. If overturned: play continues.
:::

## Kayfab-Friendly Hints {#hints}

:::rules
Kayfab-Finger Rule | Point at each card as you mention it. Helps beginners, kids, and keeps the storytelling moves visible.
Tempo Matters | Fast play = chaotic fun. Slow play = emotional depth. Both are valid.
Silence Is Allowed | Listening can be a move.
Overacting Is Encouraged | Big gestures, strange voices, exaggerated drama. Go for it.
Laughter Breaks Nothing | If everyone's laughing, you're playing it right.
:::

## Common Questions {#faq}

:::rules
Do I have to mention every card? | Yes. At least acknowledge each visible card. A single word is enough. The point is recognition, not analysis.
What if my story doesn't make sense? | Perfect. Coherence is optional. Connection matters more than logic.
Can I lie? | Yes. The cards don't fact-check. Make a claim, point at the card, sell it. Anyone else can call BLÖDSINN! or build on it.
What if no one calls BLÖDSINN! but I know someone forgot a card? | Then you call it. Or you don't. Both are valid.
Can I refuse to play a card? | Yes. You may pass. Once or twice, fine. Every turn? The others lose interest in your Character. Use sparingly.
What if every roll throws us into chaos? | Working as intended. Chaos is a valid game state. Sit in it. You calibrate as you go.
Can I add my own cards? | Absolutely. Blank cards exist for this reason. Create new Artifacts. Make them strange.
Is there a "correct" way to play? | No. The only rules that count are the ones you and the table build as you go. You shape MedKayfab to the group, not the other way around.
My story isn't clinically accurate. Bad? | Plausible beats perfect. The act of trying to make it plausible is the learning.
Can I invent mechanisms when I don't know one? | Yes. If you invent something wrong, someone calls KayfaBOGGLE and the gap shows. That's the point.
Can I mix decks across specialties? | Absolutely. The human body doesn't respect specialty boundaries, neither should the table.
:::

# Cards

## Powers & Cards {#powers}

**Nothing on a card is a fact to be recited.** Everything on a card is a mechanism to be performed. The card carries the name and the concept; the performance is yours.

:::dosay
DONT | "I block COX-1 and reduce thromboxane A2 synthesis by inhibiting platelet aggregation."
DO | "Every platelet in the bloodstream goes suddenly, permanently quiet. The patient's wife is on warfarin. *Nobody checked.*"
:::

The difference: the first is a pharmacology lecture. The second is a clinical scenario the people at the table will remember. If anyone calls BLÖDSINN! on the second, that's a conversation worth having. Nobody calls BLÖDSINN! on the first because it is already dead.

# Learning

## Learning Add-Ons {#addons}

MedKayfab is a complete game without these. Add them when the table wants more structure, or when a specific learning goal needs targeting.

:::rules
Anki Mode | After each Finale, each player writes one card: front = Character name + one clinical clue, back = mechanism in one sentence. Stack the cards. Review before the next session. You built your own Anki deck.
Feynman Round | After any Hand-Off success, the active player explains the mechanism without jargon, as if talking to the patient's family. One minute. If the group follows: Quest Die +1 bonus on the next turn.
Spaced Repetition | Shuffle 3-5 marked cards from previous sessions into the deck. When one comes up: explain it from memory before the Intro Ritual. Success: +1 Quest Die. The card you forgot last week is the card you remember this week.
Peer Teaching Round | After each Finale, roll D6. Highest = teacher. Three minutes, one card, mechanism + clinical relevance. Each other player asks one question. The teacher answers or admits not-knowing, both count.
Complication Deck | Shuffle a second deck of complication cards face-down beside the main deck. Whenever the Quest Die moves to 5, draw the top complication card and add it to the Stage. The Finale now has to address both the Quest and the complication.
Solo Mode | Play all five steps alone. For Hand-Off (Mode 3): voice the mechanism out loud, then check the card. BLÖDSINN! is your own standard. Did you actually explain the mechanism, or did you hand-wave it? Be honest. The exam will not be.
:::

# Disclaimer

## A note from Doc FrizzleBob {#disclaimer}

Trust your medical training and primary sources before you trust a comic card. Card texts may be outdated, abbreviated, or wrong, some were drafted by AI and miss nuance the way only AI can.

MedKayfab is not a fact source. It is a reasoning gym. The point isn't that the card is correct. The point is that you stood up at the table and explained the mechanism out loud, and somebody at the table either followed you or called BLÖDSINN!

If you spot an error: call BLÖDSINN! at the table, fix it in front of everyone, and tell us at *cancelthiscomic.substack.com* so we can sharpen the deck. Errors caught and corrected at the table teach more than errors that quietly stay.
