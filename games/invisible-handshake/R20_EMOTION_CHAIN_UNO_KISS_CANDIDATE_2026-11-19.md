# The Invisible Handshake · R20 · Fast HATE / FEAR / HOPE card chain

**19 November 2026 · Georg direction / separate gameplay candidate, NOT an approved replacement.** Existing owner: `games/invisible-handshake/`, existing branch `design/invisible-handshake-paper-r0-2026-11-17`, Draft PR #475. Trigger: Georg finds R19's dice, Focus ownership and extensive setup too complex, and explicitly proposes an UNO-like rapid card game **without card numbers**, in which participants must immediately explain how the played card causes **HATE, FEAR or HOPE** — only later recognizing the consequences of their own argument.

## Core insight

**PLAY one card, GIVE one specific causal sentence, advance the emotion**. In contrast to R19, no dice faces, no positional action menu, no capture arithmetic, no FOCUS claim payout, no NPC automation, no bonus for performance. **This is an A/B architecture challenger to the current R19 game**, never silently stacked atop R17–R19. The old hex/tarot may return as an optional *retrospective* of the last seven played cards, NOT as a required seven-position turn layout.

The 56-card product limit may be reused: six identity/faction cards for optional character perspectives, fifty narrative cards in the communal gameplay deck. No completed final 50-card wording or game balance is claimed. Narratives keep a strong named subject, a PUBLIC CLAIM and a PRIVATE INCENTIVE. **No numbers printed on cards, no strength values or fixed printed emotion suits.** One card may make sense as HATE, FEAR or HOPE depending on the story being constructed.

## Minimal paper experiment (one chosen primary variant)

**Setup:** 2–6 players. Shuffle 50 narrative proxy cards, deal **five** to each person. Reveal one face-up context card to begin the common trail; do not count it as a player's play. Current requested emotion starts at HATE. The six faction identities can be dealt for flavor but do not alter the rules. Remaining cards form one draw pile. This is a **new test setup**, not R19's 3-hand/6-public/Focus stock formula.

**Turn:** Play **exactly one** hand card to the shared trail. Say one short clause anchored to an actual title, object or printed detail of the card, and connect it to the preceding card or center topic. Pattern:
- **HATE:** “They blame [CARD], because [card-specific detail].”
- **FEAR:** “That makes [CARD] frightening, because [specific effect].”
- **HOPE:** “But [CARD] promises a cure, because [claim].”

A title-only causal fragment is sufficient for novice play, provided it names the card and a consequence. There is **no other player/judge vote on cleverness or plausibility**. When the player cannot or does not wish to make a link, the player **draws exactly one** if possible and ends their turn without advancing emotion. This is a voluntary risk/cost, not a performative elimination criterion. No simultaneous interruption or challenge in this first test.

**Automatic resolution:** A successful card moves the current emotion to the next position: **HATE → FEAR → HOPE → HATE**. On HOPE, finish the three-card mini-story by reading that played card's **PRIVATE INCENTIVE**: who actually profits if the alleged cure is sold? These three cards remain visible in a three-card episode record (a common discard/played area), ready for the next episode. No ownership/trophy accounting. The private text is not a supernatural truth claim; it is fiction. Reading the reversal must be short enough not to interrupt the rhythm.

**Win / stop:** The **first human to empty their hand after a successful play wins immediately**. On empty draw pile, any player who cannot play passes; if an entire human circuit passes without a played card, end the game and the fewest held cards wins (tie = shared win). No NPCs are required for low player counts; with 2 humans play simply alternates. Six players play one card per turn without waiting for NPC reactions.

**No hidden wording requirement:** Cards may display both PUBLIC CLAIM and PRIVATE INCENTIVE; the private text is **read at the HOPE reveal**, not physically hidden or treated as inaccessible. A future graphic treatment may emphasize the reveal, but no special back or sleeve is necessary for the first paper test.

## Constructed three-card mini-story — concrete reused R17 subjects

1. **HATE:** `THE LEAK`. “Hate: THE LEAK releases information according to its owner's schedule, so the wrong people become the public enemy.”
2. **FEAR:** `THE BELL`. “Fear: THE BELL turns that scandal into an alarm that keeps emergency powers running.”
3. **HOPE:** `THE SAFETY ADVISORY`. “Hope: THE SAFETY ADVISORY promises protection from the danger.” Then read its existing PRIVATE INCENTIVE: **“Protection is billed by the remaining threat level.”**

What people thought they played: scandal → public warning → safety. What the three cards reveal: a managed disclosure, a renewal contract, and a cure that depends on the problem persisting. The satire is the **unplanned systemic contradiction** exposed by the actual played cards, not an imposed gag or mandatory performance scoring.

## Game-theory risks and measurable decisions

- If **any card** can satisfy **any emotion** with a generic phrase, hand selection is strategically vacuous. The test must record whether a **specific printed motif** influenced a choice. **Fail** if removing individual titles/art doesn't change plays.
- If people hesitate for more than a few seconds to justify a card, the game will feel like an oral exam. Target a single short clause and no judicial evaluation, but **actual timing is unknown**.
- If the PRIVATE INCENTIVE is visible upfront, a later reveal may be weak; if it is hidden, a new print-production complexity appears. Test audible end-of-HOPE reread first; no hidden-information design assumed.
- A pure first-empty-hand race could favor lucky initial cards or trivialize play. Measure it before adding special actions, skip/reverse tokens, counting or secret goals.
- One emotion per play is a **concrete forced constraint** despite lack of printed numbers. Alternative where a player freely chooses H/F/H is a later controlled challenger, because it risks removing the only creative constraint.
- Satirical reading is **voluntary beyond the minimal causal clause**: no player must perform a monologue, identify a historical conspiracy or demonstrate esoteric knowledge.
- **Optional postgame:** put the last seven played narratives in the existing Tarot hex and read their public claim/private motive as an absurd after-action report; this has **zero gameplay consequence** and must not slow the UNO-speed turn.
