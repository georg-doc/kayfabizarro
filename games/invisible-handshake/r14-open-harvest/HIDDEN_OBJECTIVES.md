# Hidden objectives · proposal, not tested

User intent: simple private goals, uncertain alliances, partial cooperation and later divergence; avoid experienced-player-only advantages. Random assignment does not establish equal difficulty or equal winning chances.

First candidate for a later controlled test: three symmetric goal families, two copies each if six objective slips are needed: personally harvest twice via Fear, Hope or Hate. Each has identical threshold and rule wording; permuting emotion labels should preserve opportunities in a symmetric ruleset. Duplicate goals are allowed and can create overlap or conflict. This is not a promised balanced win threshold or an accepted additional deck allocation. Objective slips/app assignment need not consume six more narrative cards from the56-card product.

Do not yet combine unlike targets (three harvests versus a rare card combination versus burning opponents' narratives). Their effort depends on card availability, seats, player count and hostility. Symmetric labels also cease to be equivalent once faction powers or card-specific emotion effects appear.

For testing, measure first-completion rates/times across all assignments, seat rotations and2–6 counts; compare against the same public scoring baseline. Evaluate whether a player must memorize several opposing missions. Randomness does not remove expertise advantages; readable private goals can reduce onboarding load but experienced players still infer incentives.

Critical limitation: hidden goals do not by themselves fix the deterministic5→6 gift. A player trying to harvest Fear will still harvest an offered Fear6 before its preparer returns. Resolve preparation incentives before treating secret missions as a balance remedy. A later prototype should choose explicitly between alternative win conditions and modest endgame bonuses; do not silently mix them.
