## title
FORGET UTOPIA: Fifty-Six Cards for counting what has to be true

## subtitle
A free Cut & Play Comic about the Pitch that keeps selling you Tomorrow, and the one Question that makes it sweat.

## body
Somebody at your table owns a gadget that was supposed to change their life. Ask them when it did. Watch them start the answer twice.

That pause is what this deck is about. FORGET UTOPIA is the first of three free decks in Uncle FrizzleBob's Three Futures set: Forget Utopia, Ignore Dystopia, Embrace Protopia. Nobody here argues that the future is bad. You get fifty-six names for the moves people use when they sell you one, and a table of friends to try them on.

**How it plays**

Print the PDF. Cut along the lines. Shuffle. Then take turns telling one shared story, card by card, while the person next to you judges whether your move holds. Nobody wins. Nobody loses. At some point you will defend a pitch you despised ten minutes earlier, because the story needs a salesman and you drew the card.

**What you end up saying out loud**

You walk down the showroom in fourteen pages, four cards each. Early on you draw The Glossy Horizon: "Hold up a future so polished the visitor sees their own face in it. Sell the reflection." Whoever holds it has to actually sell. Out loud. To their friends. The first time you do it, you hear how little it takes.

Then The Signature Line: "Offer the dream. Ask only that they stop looking at the floor and sign." Its lore line is the sharpest sentence in the deck: awe and scrutiny use different states of mind, and awe is faster. At the table this turns into a small duel of timing. The seller tries to close before the skeptic finds the floor. The skeptic tries to look down.

Halfway through comes The Treadmill: "Movement without arrival, billed monthly." Expect somebody to quote it later in the week, probably while cancelling something.

**The Counter-Moves**

On the last pages you turn the pitch around. The Shine Test: the shinier it is, the harder you should look for the floor. The Load-Bearing Lie: find the one assumption that, if false, drops the whole roof. And the closing card, The Core Question: hold the brochure up to the light, count the things that have to be true, notice how many are not.

That is the whole method. One person reads a promise aloud. Somebody else counts the assumptions on their fingers. The rest of the table rules on which ones would survive an ordinary Tuesday.

**Why Cards and not an Essay**

An essay about techno-optimism asks you to agree with it. With a card in your hand you have to perform the move, and performing a pitch is the fastest way to stop being moved by one. You cannot unhear yourself selling The Velvet Rope ("Make scarcity feel like status. The line is the product.") to a friend who once queued overnight for a phone.

None of this claims that progress is fake. Some promises hold. The point is to find out which ones while you can still ask, before The Afterglow sends you home shining, with the details left for "a calmer head that never arrives."

**Three Futures**

Forget Utopia clears the ground. Next week, Ignore Dystopia takes apart the opposite sale: the end of the world as a subscription. The week after, Embrace Protopia puts a pencil in your hand. Play them apart or shuffle them together; the same question gets three answers.
