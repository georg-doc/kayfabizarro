## title
EMBRACE PROTOPIA: Fifty-Six Cards you can actually do this Week

## subtitle
A free Cut & Play Comic for People who start before they are ready.

## body
Ask the person next to you what is broken within reach of their arm right now. Not in the world. In the room. Give them ten seconds. Most people find something: a wobbly chair, a stuck drawer, a message to a neighbour they never answered.

That is the whole game. EMBRACE PROTOPIA is the last of Uncle FrizzleBob's Three Futures decks. In Forget Utopia you took the brochure off the wall. In Ignore Dystopia you asked who benefits from the alarm. Here you pick up a pencil. Protopia is Kevin Kelly's word for a future that gets a little better each day instead of arriving finished, and every card in this deck is sized to fit inside one of those days.

**How it plays**

Print the PDF. Cut along the lines. Shuffle. Take turns telling one shared story, card by card, while the person next to you judges whether your move would hold up off the page. Nobody wins. Nobody loses. The quiet person at the end of the table will propose the smallest move of the evening, and it will be the one everybody remembers.

**What you end up saying out loud**

The first page is called The Sketch, and its header reads: "Finished is for tombstones. Draw it in pencil." Then The Ugly First Draft: "Make the first version bad on purpose. Bad is how you know it is real." People play this one as a dare. Somebody describes the worst possible first version of a thing they actually want to do, and everyone else gets to improve it exactly once.

The Held Breath: "Before reacting, take the one breath the feed is built to deny you." Try playing it literally. Whoever draws it stops the round for one slow exhale. The first time, people laugh. By the third time, they wait for it.

The Awkward Start: "Go to the first small, clumsy gathering anyway. Clumsy is the entry fee." The Nearest Lever: "Look around the actual room you are in. Find the one thing within arm's reach." The Compounding: "One repair a week is fifty-two a year. Nobody clapped, and the place is different."

**Why Pencil**

The pages look like field notes: pencil, watercolour, crossed-out lines, deliberately unfinished, so nobody at the table feels they have to be finished either. There is even a card about what this costs you. The Worthwhile Bill: "Pay in certainty and comfort. Get back the wheel the other two decks took." You give up the comfort of knowing how it ends. You get your hands back.

The last card closes all three decks at once. THE CORE QUESTION: "Forget the brochure. Ignore the loop. Pick up the pencil."

**Three Futures**

That is the set. Forget Utopia, Ignore Dystopia, Embrace Protopia. Play them in order and you go from "out of your hands" to "built by hand". Play them shuffled and watch your friends argue about which future a card belongs to.
