## title
IGNORE DYSTOPIA: Fifty-Six Cards for asking who benefits when you stop

## subtitle
A free Cut & Play Comic about the End of the World sold as a Subscription, and the Question you ask before you forward the next Alarm.

## body
Somebody at your table read the news before breakfast and has been tired ever since. Ask them what they did about what they read. Most of us give the same answer: nothing, but with feeling.

IGNORE DYSTOPIA, subtitled Anatomy of a Trap, is the second of Uncle FrizzleBob's Three Futures decks. Last week, in Forget Utopia, you named the moves that sell a finished paradise. Here you name the moves that sell a finished catastrophe. Both sellers need the same thing from you: that you stop moving.

**How it plays**

Print the PDF. Cut along the lines. Shuffle. Take turns telling one shared story, card by card, while the person next to you judges whether your move holds. Nobody wins. Nobody loses. Somebody will end up performing the loudest pundit in the room and discover they are good at it.

**What you end up saying out loud**

You start at The Doomsday Clock: "Set a public timer near midnight and move the hands by announcement, never by event." Whoever plays it gets to stand up and move the hands. Then The Frozen Watcher, an opossum melting into an armchair in front of a television: "Convert the citizen into an audience by giving them a clock they cannot stop." People at a table recognise the armchair before they recognise the opossum.

The middle pages are about your body. The Cortisol Economy: "Sell the cortisol spike, then sell the relief, to the exact same customer." Read that one aloud and watch somebody reach for their phone, then put it face down.

Then the arithmetic. The Missing Denominator: "Show the scary number; hide what it is out of." Try a house rule with it: nobody at the table may react to a statistic until someone has asked "out of how many?" By the second round people start asking before they are told to.

**The Way out**

The last cards are tools you take home. The Loop Test: watch whether the story ever resolves; if it only renews, you found the mechanism. The Beneficiary Map: trace who gets paid while you stay still. And THE CORE QUESTION: "Ask this of every alarm before you forward it: who benefits when you stop acting?"

Nobody at this table is told the dangers are fake. The lore on the last card is careful about that: the collapse may be real, but separating genuine danger from manufactured stillness is the cure. This is media literacy played as satire. None of the fifty-six card texts names a person, a party or a company. You are not building a theory about who is behind it. You are practising one question until you ask it without thinking.

**Three Futures**

Forget Utopia cleared the ground last week. Ignore Dystopia is the middle panel, where you walk past the alarm instead of freezing in front of it. Next week, Embrace Protopia puts a pencil in your hand. Play them apart or shuffle them together.
