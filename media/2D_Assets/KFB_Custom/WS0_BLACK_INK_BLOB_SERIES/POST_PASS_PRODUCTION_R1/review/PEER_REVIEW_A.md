## Tone, cultural claims and limits

The sampled texts use individual domestic/professional mechanisms, dry jokes and fallible motives. They do not turn the cultural design references into factual ethnographic claims. Props are often used metaphorically, which is acceptable DESIGN lore but is not evidence that a final image actually obeys its prompt. All sampled paired names and possessions matched their supplied identities.

Several biographies resolve tension through a quiet humane exception. That is a recognisable catalogue rhythm and may merit whole-book editorial tuning, but it is not in itself a prompt-fidelity defect and this limited review does not grant or withhold global text approval.

A global keyword scan helped locate posture/transfer language outside the original sample; that scan is **not** a full close reading of all remaining identities. Review used supplied prompt excerpts/personality lines and not full remote prompts or rendered images. In particular, “no directed handoff found” means no such action in the reviewed InternationalCouples texts, not a visual certification of the source sheets. Final combined-catalogue review and Georg's text acceptance remain separate gates.
