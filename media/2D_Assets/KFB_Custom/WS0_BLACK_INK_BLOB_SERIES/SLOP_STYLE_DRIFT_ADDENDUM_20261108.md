## Five checks before another batch

1. **Eyes.** Check the visible sclera at source and game size. The default is warm white sclera, a distinct red iris and a black pupil. Closed lids and occlusion are legitimate. Record any intentional colored sclera as a character or family decision. A tinted visor deserves a close look before calling an eye red.
2. **Acting.** Mark gaze, lids and mouth for each cell independently of the prop. Check neighboring cells and related sheets. Repeated scowls with the same gaze and open fanged mouth trigger review. Shared mood or uniform can still work with different mouth shapes, degrees of tension and gaze targets.
3. **Tips and silhouette.** Include tapered splash points, hooks, curls, small lobes and rounded masses across the line. The legacy Teen Punk Rebel demonstrates a pointed red crest, and Aliens show long appendages that grow from the mass. These are examples of useful silhouette extremes, not identification of a specifically named original “spikey” sheet. Several early production prompts explicitly excluded spiky rock or fur forms. Preserve that material distinction and allow organic ink tapers. Avoid turning the rounded school correction into a universal broad base.
4. **Ink color and texture.** Compare on matched neutral, light and dark backgrounds. The old Alien bodies read darker and cooler than the saturated purple Magic effects, and the Royals carry stronger magenta costume highlights. Mechanics and the quiet Ambivalence set are useful style references for dark ink with subtle purple undertones and controlled sheen. Check for pale rims, plastic shine, fabric-like grain and strong purple body fill. Do this visually before assuming palette drift from an accent, shading or transparent edge.
5. **Props.** Judge whether the item reads at game size, explains the role or joke and uses the right material. Early mechanics and builders show how an oversized tool can carry the action. Multiple similar hand-held panels weaken role separation in Card Containment. The granular water bottle in Hitchhiking #194 calls for clear plastic and water highlights. Keep the strong shapes of helmets, tools and accessories, and vary material finish according to what each prop is made of.

Next gate is a one-character comparison of the legacy pneumatic-drill worker, with the exact roster ID and source preserved. Check original and candidate at 64/96 px and catalog size. One variable per comparison gives the owner a readable choice before any set-scale regeneration or version selection.
